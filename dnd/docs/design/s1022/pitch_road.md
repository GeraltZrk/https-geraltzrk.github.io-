# 《长路问山》 The Long Road to Trialmount

Campaign pitch for s1022 (1022 AA). Angle: an adventuring company's long road. Citations: `[wiki: Page]`, `[ck3: path under src/ck3/]`. "Bookmark events" = `localization/english/event_localization/anb_bookmark_events_l_english.yml`.

## 1. Title, logline, stakes

**中文标题**　《长路问山》
**English title**　*The Long Road to Trialmount*

**Logline (中文).** 1022 年开春，仗打完了。安本科斯特的营帐一顶顶拆掉，领不到饷的老兵凑成一支没有旗号的兵团，接了桩替神殿跑腿的差事：把当年登上试炼山的几个活人挨个找到，问清楚山顶上到底出了什么事。这一问，从东坎诺的沼泽问到小人国的染坊，又问到布勒瓦尔太阳王的花园。十八年后，白色瘟疫从渡鸦山的坟里爬出来。团里的人类老了，精灵没变，当年捡来的孩子成了全坎诺最叫人害怕的法师。最后大家回到那座山下，替整片大陆答一句话：往后这世上，谁说了算。

**Logline (English).** A company of paid-off veterans with no banner takes a clerk's errand from the Seventh Pantheonic Council: find the survivors of Trialmount and write down what happened on the summit. The errand walks them across Cannor and into Bulwar, then waits eighteen years for the plague to prove what they learned.

**World-scale stake.** Trialmount still witnesses oaths, and whoever mends its Wound in the plague year decides who Cannor obeys afterwards: a new Castan, its mages, a compact of elves and men, nobody, or a lich whose subjects are a hundred thousand bound dead. On the way there the company's choices set how many the White Pestilence kills, who is blamed for surviving it, and whether "Anbennar" is remembered as a promise or as a slur.

**Emotional spine.** A table of people with no clan grows old at different speeds: the humans gain eighteen years, the elf gains none, and the child they fed becomes the most dangerous adult in the room. Killing the lich costs one seat at that table, and canon already knows who pays if nobody at yours will.

## 3. The truth, and when the player learns it

**Truth.** The Trials are one old working: the mountain witnesses a ruler's oath and Castanor's stones remember it. Nichmer, whose art was seizing other people's magic [wiki: Nichmer, the Sorcerer-King], went up to seize that one and turn the oath round, so that the people would be bound to the ruler. Clarimonde killed him halfway through a sentence. The sentence is still hanging on the Oathstone: that is the Wound.

Canrec knows what his master attempted. His plague does not aim at killing; it enrols. Every victim is stitched into the Book, and a man who carries a hundred thousand sworn souls up a mountain that witnesses oaths comes down as a Castan nobody alive agreed to. His reason is the Oath itself: "to defend my land and my people to the end of my days". He has no end of days, and his people will never starve, rebel or be enthralled again. Elves resist the plague because it was bred on human dead for decades before any elf landed; he did not plan that, but he has let the rumour run that elves made it.

Nothing else is a conspiracy. The purists, Ioriel, Garion, the Ravenseers all act for their own reasons.

| End of | The player believes |
|---|---|
| Prologue | Easy money: twelve depositions, three seals each. |
| Act 1 | Nichmer was *doing* something up there and it is not finished. Someone else has been asking after the emperor's body. Suspects: Garrec, the harpy, Iacob the Betrayer. |
| Act 2 | Garion told the Council the Trials are beyond repair, and lied. A living enchanter is still using Nichmer's craft, and the company's own child has the same gift. |
| Act 3 | Jaher: "A man does not run uphill." Nichmer climbed to be crowned. Iacob is innocent and frightened. Canrec's grave is empty, his laboratory is forty years deep, and no one will pay to hunt a dead man. |
| Act 4 | The plague is an electorate. The dead are walking to Trialmount. |
| Finale | Unmaking the Book needs one person to walk into it. The half-sentence on the Oathstone will accept any ending spoken by someone who passed the Trials. |

## 5. Act by act

**Prologue. Anbenncóst, 1–9 Castanmark 1022.** Per origin: an opening that shows the wound, two check scenes, one small fight (dock thieves, a press-gang, stockade guards, a ration mob, the minder's men), a first meeting with Cora, then The Pot.

### Act 1. Escann, Esmarment–Suren 1022: *The Dead Emperor's Road*

**Question:** whose is the body of a dead empire?

1. Bladeskeep. Queen Clarimonde (23): "He wasn't fighting at the end. He was talking to the stone. I interrupted a sentence." A Black Castanorian prisoner is about to draw Calindal: worthy, or blind.
2. Canreced. The obvious lead is a fat count who has been burned in effigy twice.
3. Bal Mire. Brohnfeld's watchtower; the cleric of Nerat admits another client looked into his bowl last autumn. Vacera is here, digging up legionaries to count how they died.
4. The War to End the Dragon. Garrec (24) under siege by Reachmen, Adeanic zealots and kin-traitors.
5. Headman's Wood, the waystation, the stew. Cora nearly fails the old man's test.
6. Under the tile: Castan LIII and the Ebonsteel Crown.
7. North Citadel. Brok's ledger: "Ten Trials. Seven and a half broken." A sentry who cursed on the slope has been unable to lie since.

**Big choices.** *The siege:* fight for Garrec (Black Dragon up, Mantle +1), for the coalition (Ebonmarck partitioned, Garrec dead as in the mod's lose state), or force a Trial by Chivalry (Sapphire Eye up, Roll +1). *The body:* Westgate (dynastic), North Citadel (imperial), Pantheonway (for all Castanor), or back under the tile; and who holds the crown. Mantle moves −2 to +2; it decides who can reach the body in 1042.
**Fights:** werewolves at night (special: feed the fire with the baggage); Ebonmarck's gate (specials: the frozen ford, the siege ladder). **Level 4.**
*Options by seat:* Ebonmarck origin is known at Bal Mire; elves see the bowl's working; Sapphire Eye can stand as champion; a cleric may bury the bog's dead.

### Act 2. Small Country, Lencenor, Dameshead, Yshdament–Truefrost 1022: *The Winners' Table*

**Question:** can the victors still eat together?

1. Viswall. King Finnic (20) confesses he pocketed a chip of the Oathstone, "habit". Every promise made in the room where it lies gets kept, which has ruined halfling politics. Lorent and Gawed both want it.
2. The Redglades. Ioriel keeps the anniversary of Ruben's death. The company carries Crege Dameris's blood-price, and she decides whether an accident can be paid for.
3. Enteben. Roen Arthelis, whose "elvenized" brother died at Morban Flats, hears purist preachers in his own hall.
4. Brok's clan offer.
5. Seinathíl. Alarian Singkeep names Cora's gift. A petty enchanter has kept a baron compelled since the Grand Ball to win a boundary suit.
6. The Moonmount. The Council takes the depositions: Caylen recognised, Alaria refused unless Vacera speaks. Garion, one-armed, admits in private that the mountain can be mended.
7. King Marion (21), after supper: "Could a half-elf climb it?"

**Big choices.** *The enchanter:* the mob's rope (Tower −2), the king's court (0), or the Wise's custody under geas (Tower +2). *Garion's lie:* expose it (Mantle +2, the Wise down) or keep it (Tower +1). *The blood-price:* Rose and Moon ±2. *Cora:* send her to Seinathíl, bind her, or hide her.
**Fights:** the dyeworks brawl against Gawedi "customs men" (special: tip the indigo vat); the baron's hall, where the guards are compelled (special: break the working instead of the men). **Level 6.**

### Act 3. Ibevar, Corvuria, Bulwar, 1023–1024: *The Bride's Road*

**Question:** what do liberators owe the liberated, and what did the Sorcerer-King leave at home?

1. Vardoced. Ibenion, calm and purist, hires the company to take Lanahari to the Sun King: elves should marry elves.
2. Arca Corvur. Governor Denarion and Kolvan Karnid; Korbarid rebels ambush the train for a hostage.
3. Ravenhill. The Ravenseers' inner council shows the Unkind dream: a white king over a silent country. One wing wants Cora dead tonight.
4. The Dreadmire. Gnolls; a temple of Surael with a sealed door underneath, and a Roilsardi knight of the escort who very much wants elven years.
5. The laboratory. Jars, ledgers back to 980, and something in a pit that has not lain down since 1009.
6. Bulwar. Gardens behind walls, temple-banks, harpy captives from Siadan in the menagerie. Jaher gives his deposition and offers every veteran a legion in the east.
7. Muster-out. Agreed signal: when the dead walk, come back to the pot.

**Big choices.** *The laboratory:* burn it (Pestilence −1 now, but the cure is two years late: +3 in Act 4), seal it, carry samples to Garion and Vacera (cure by 1041; risk track), or give it to the Ravenseers. *The tomb:* leave it, or let the knight look (vampires come early, epilogue). *The rebels:* Communion and Small Crowns. *Jaher's offer:* Castan and the Roll.
**Fights:** the pass (special: cut the bridal wagon loose); the pit (special: fire the jars, which destroys the samples). **Level 8.**

**Interlude, 1024–1040.** Four choice scenes: where you wintered, whom you wrote to, what the company hall became, and the comet night of Tearfall 1033 when Cora, 25, lights the whole camp without meaning to. Humans return grey; elves and `is.immortal` leads are met by people who no longer recognise them. Ioriel turns one hundred and the elders must stop calling her a child.

### Act 4. Corvuria, the Borders, Gawed, 1040–1042: *The Pestilence Road*

**Question:** who is blamed, and who gets the cure?

1. Ravenhill, 3 Castanmark 1040. The open grave; the sun-elf garrison stays healthy and the town notices.
2. Re-muster. Who answers depends on the Roll and on what you did to them. A squire of thirteen, "Dres", carries your shield.
3. The Necropolis of Corseton. Ten thousand corseted dead standing straight in their niches; the tollwrights will not burn them.
4. Duke Dyren (27) shuts the Borders to elves and everyone else.
5. Varlosen. The first thousand doses.
6. Alenath, 1042. Cora can send the mob home.
7. Merescker. Edmund Fairhand falls and gets up.
8. The dead turn north-east together.

**Big choices.** *Necropolis:* burn (Pestilence −2, Small Crowns down), hold and evacuate, or trust the rites. *Doses:* the lines, the cities, the poor, or the highest bidder (Pestilence, Communion, Rose and Moon). *Alenath:* Cora dominates the crowd (Communion +1, Tower +3, and every mage-hater is proved right), you stand in front of the torches (check; you may lose the quarter), or evacuate by the back lanes (the quarter burns as in canon, the elves live).
**Fights:** the catacombs; Palehand (he remembers Act 1); the Alenath riot if it goes wrong. **Level 10.**
*By race:* elves walk the plague wards freely and are stoned for it; humans and halflings roll for every bite.

### Finale. Trialmount, 31 Tearfall 1042 (ten scenes, the Trials in order)

1. **The rally cry.** Who answers from the plain depends on reputations and the Roll. Canrec cried at dawn and a hundred thousand answered.
2. **The map-desk.** Draw Cannor as it is, then "as it SHALL BE". The line you draw opens or closes endings.
3. **The forge.** Brok, and a gift for the dwarves.
4. **The Oathstone.** Nichmer's half-sentence.
5. **The crown of mankind.** Each people adds a stone; Finnic's chip comes home.
6. **Two forest spirits,** feuding over the damage.
7. **The wyvern.** Castan's trial.
8. **Jump round a rock ten times.** The one Trial that survived intact. Canrec had to do it too.
9. **The golem.** Disarm it and it kneels until you die.
10. **The summit.** Canrec, the Book, the price, the oath.

**The price:** a companion who loves you enough volunteers, or you go, or nobody goes and Canrec breaks for the east face. **The oath** selects the ending.

## 7. Endings

1. **瓦下 Under the Tile** (buried; bittersweet; the canonical baseline). The emperor is walled in with the Oathstone. No Castan again. Each realm buries its own; Cast and Anor drift toward Castellyr. Default when no other oath qualifies. With Communion ≤ 3 it reads as *each to his own fire*: elfrealms behind walls, and Gawed learns to say "outsider".
2. **第五十四位 The Fifty-Fourth** (restored). Garrec, Castan, or Marion as the Silver Dragon swears the whole Oath. Legions and decrees shorten the plague. The west looks east and counts spears. Needs Mantle ≥ 6, a living willing claimant, and the body where they can reach it.
3. **守山人 The Watch on the Mountain** (mages as guardians). The Wise swear the Oath as an order and take the geas themselves: "will not bring unjust strife". Cora goes first, by choice. Needs Tower 4–7 and the Wise's trust.
4. **法师的和平 The Mages' Peace** (mages as masters; the mantle stolen; the defensible wrong). Cora or Garion finishes Nichmer's sentence with kinder words. Kings obey the quarantine. It works, for two centuries. Tower ≥ 8, or tell Cora that Alenath was right.
5. **同席 Anbennar** (communion). No emperor: elves, men, dwarves and halflings each put a hand on the stone and promise to bury each other's dead. Slow, leaky, real. Needs Communion ≥ 7, four peoples answering the cry, and Rose and Moon below blood.
6. **寂静之冠 The Quiet Crown** (stolen by you; tragic and defensible). You take Canrec's mark and order the dead to lie down. They do, all of them, that night. A hundred thousand souls now wait on your word, and a squire will be told what you are.
7. **不肯落座的神** (divine; `is.god` only). The mountain witnesses a god and has no oath that fits. The next Pantheonic Council must vote on you while you are in the room.

**Epilogue cards** look forward: the price paid or deferred ("It took thirteen more years. The squire's name was Aldresia."); the Ioriellan decades by Rose and Moon; whether the Interregnum finds a word like *Anbennar* waiting or has to invent it in 1221; the Lilac Wars as the feud you fed; Castellyr or a living Castanor when the orcs come; Ettalinde's door; the Ravenseers; each companion; each origin's land.

## 9. Risks, and disagreements with the brief

- **The mountain "witnesses".** Canon says the Trials had magic and that it was "drained, severed". Treating the Oathstone as one oath-working is our reading. Defuse: nobody in the text explains it with authority. Garion, Brok, Jaher and the Ravenseers give four accounts that almost agree.
- **Canon people in the party and on stage.** Castan LIV can die or be crowned; Aldresia appears as a child. Castan has traits and claims but no written story; Aldresia is a stub. We keep the squire nameless until the epilogue so that her canon stays intact if the player defers the price.
- **Killing Canrec in 1042.** Only if someone pays. Otherwise he escapes and 1053 stands. The cure still comes from his laboratory unless the player burned it, and then it is late, not absent.
- **Garion as a liar and a possible master.** He becomes a reluctant emperor in 1238, so a careful, frightened Garion fits. Keep him sympathetic and never a schemer.
- **Engine load.** Six lands in four acts. Each act gets two hub locations and travel montage scenes with one check each; five prologues share a final scene. Companions need two portraits and two stat blocks (1022 and 1040); Cora's voice change is worth the cost. Harpy must be allowed as a companion race even if it is not offered to players.
- **God mode.** A god in 1022 lands on the Seventh Council's agenda between Munas and Alaria, which is the best joke the era offers; the golem and Canrec need `noAwe`; the Quiet Crown must stay tempting even for a god (the dead obey the mark, not divinity).
- **Tone.** The Necropolis and Alenath are grim. The narration should not wink at them. The laughs belong to Finnic's honest city council, the count of Canreced and the rock.

**Disagreements.**
1. *Lorent and Gawed as origins.* I left them out. They are the two courts the company petitions, and a player who belongs to one stops being a guest at every table. Rose and Moon gets its own track instead.
2. *"Who at the Ball is still enthralled?"* I kept this small: one petty enchanter and a boundary suit. A grand domination plot would turn the era back into a dark-lord story. The danger of the gift is carried by Cora, whom the player has fed since scene four.
3. *Time span.* Acts 1–3 run 1022–1024, so the skip on screen is sixteen years (eighteen from the prologue). Stretching Act 3 to 1026 buys nothing.
4. *First blood of Rose and Moon.* It was shed in 1020 in the lists. The player cannot prevent the feud, only price it.
