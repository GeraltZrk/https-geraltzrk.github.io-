# s1820 pitch — "crowns" angle

## 1. Title, logline, stakes

**《空王座》 / The Empty Thrones** (灰元1820—1838)

**Logline.** On New Year's Day 1820 a scholar rows out of a whirlpool with a pixie in a jar, and every chancery in Cannor works out the same three things at once: the comet that fed magic broke up ten years ago, the planes are open, and nobody owns either. Over eighteen years you rise from somebody's clerk, fixer, veteran or registered property to the one person every power owes a favour — the hand on the Republic's referendums, a queen's talking crown, a general's stolen train, an emperor's deathbed and a wedding that would marry God to the Door — and at last you stand at the locked gate of Heaven, where there is one more empty chair.

**一句话梗概。** 灰元1820年元旦，一个学者划着小船从大漩涡里钻了出来。他怀里抱着几盆会发光的草，还有一只装在果酱罐里的小妖精。同一天，坎诺各国的衙门都算明白了三件事。喂养魔法的彗星十年前就碎了。位面的门开了。这两样东西眼下都没有主人。往后十八年，你从某位大人物的跟班、打手、老兵，混成了屋里说话算数的那个人。也可能你起初只是一件登记在册的财产。共和国的公投要过你的手。女王头上那顶会说话的王冠、将军抢来的火车、老皇帝的病床，也一样。还有一桩婚事，能把“神”和“门”凑成一家。最后你站到上了锁的天门跟前。那儿还空着一把椅子。

**World-scale stake.** Who ends the 1830s owning what is left of magic and the doors out of the world: a treaty of five powers, a church, a chartered company, one armed republic, a restored emperor — or nobody, because it was allowed to run out. And which thrones of Cannor are still occupied in 1838 (the Dove Throne, the Ruby Crown, the League's council table, the Vivin masquerade, the High Rector's chair), by whom, and whether anything is invited back through Heaven's gate.

**Emotional spine.** You keep being handed other people's crowns to give away by people who assume you have no stake of your own; the campaign asks what you owe the ones you crowned and the ones you passed over. Four friends each love a different master — a queen, a republic, a search for God, a people who are legally furniture — and you cannot keep all four promises.

## 3. The truth and the reveal schedule

**The truth.** The three wonders of the age are one bill. Every black cartridge pushes a little magic out of the world for good, and a decade of industrial nullification has thinned the walls where the Astral Tree touches Halann. That is why a tuning fork opened the Root in 1820, why breaches leak, and why damestear cores have started to wake. Every general staff has the figures and keeps buying bullets, because black powder is also the only thing that stops mages ruling again — and they are right about that too. The Fragment has repeated one two-word phrase since 1810; the code is old, a waking mechanim can simply hear it, and the Veridicals have sat on it for a generation because it reads either "close the door" or "the door is closed on me". Canon's question (god, prisoner, recording?) is never answered; the player chooses a reading. And the last heir to the Dove Throne did not die in 1810: the palace went somewhere. He is on Heaven's doorstep with Torrieth, and whoever fetches him home holds the most dangerous claim in Cannor.

**What the player believes**
- *After the prologue:* a scholar found fairyland and my employers want in.
- *After Act 1:* ordinary politics — referendums, a queen who argues with herself, a race for Busilar's frequencies. Odd notes: the Crown has a tenant; the Republic buried a report on "wastage".
- *After Act 2:* the shortage is man-made and speeding up; bullets are the leak; the Palace was not destroyed but moved.
- *After Act 3:* doors and wakers have the same cause; God has been saying one thing and the Church hid it; there is a frequency for the Celestial shore.
- *After Act 4:* I have seen the lock. I know what each way of opening costs, and that nobody at the Summit can be trusted with all of it. That includes me.

## 5. Outline

*Each numbered beat below is two or three scenes; acts run 16–20.*

**Prologue (per origin, 5–6 scenes, level 2).** A dated object (cable, manifest, stoppage log); your job; one problem solved by checks; one small fight; one choice that marks you with a faction's colour; orders to attend a lecture. Ricard is waiting at the door with your pass.

**Act 1 — 承诺之年 The Year of Promises.** Anbenncóst, Konwell, Lorentainé; 1820. *Whose wartime promises get kept?*
1. The lecture at the Order of Chroniclers: a pixie in a jar, eleven spies in the third row, Busilari minders who confiscate the fork; the pixie gets out.
2. Reán síl Tirufeld, tactful and two glasses in, gives you the Republic's answer to Esmaria's vote to carry: honour or annul.
3. Konwell square: the brick, the shot, the three volleys. You cannot prevent all of it; you decide who is blamed.
4. A burnt-out ministry mage levels a Forgeway works. In the wreck: the "wastage" file. General Deshird collects it personally and is strangely careless about fire.
5. Lorentainé: the levee, the Old Guard's seals, the Queen's two voices; Lisolette joins.
6. Eilís in her rest-house: sane; she heard the Crown at sixteen and said so.
7. Castíne asks you, the one person with no Lorentish stake, whether to answer the voice.
8. The price of peace: a match between the Queen and General Josep. Tearfall: roofs full of picnickers counting a thin shower.

*Choices:* honour/annul (dove ±1, guns ∓1); who carries the blame (republic/floor rep); Castíne's course — her own, Ioriel's, or the crown off for Eilís (crowns ±1, sets the Lorent flag for the whole campaign); make or wreck the match. *Fights:* minders and Bilge Syndicate men over the jar; the square (fleeable; lose = arrested); Old Guard duellists in a palace corridor. *Skip:* two years.

**Act 2 — 女巫王的账本 The Witch-Queen's Ledger.** Gaweton, the Alen line, Castonath, the Ancardian border; 1822. *Who inherits power that cannot be handed over?*
1. Changing trains at Gaweton as Brasslance crosses the Alen and rings Rathroost.
2. A coronation in a railway hotel. Carry Thornham's bribe, switch the points, or kneel to Oswald I.
3. Night line through the Greatwoods: the Woodswallers have stopped using axes.
4. Castonath: Yamina Gracosael, ninety-two, a Floodborn who has out-willed the Drowning every rainy season since 1730. This season she is tired.
5. Her ledger: yield per casting since 1810 against cartridges fired. And the scrying plate of Tearfall night: the Palace leaving.
6. Middlesday, seized contraband in the cellars of a realm that bans artifice, answers a question nobody asked it.
7. Ancardia's soldiers' parliament, famous for its brawls; Oto of Moreced wants his war before old Dalyon dies.
8. The succession: the rite to a fourteen-year-old apprentice (or to a caster PC), annexation and the hoard milled into bullets, or a Castonath Compact with the hoard under three seals.

*Choices:* king or no king in the north (crowns ±1, guns ±1); the hoard (mana ±2); Escann's shape. *Fights:* werewolves on the carriage roof; the parliamentary brawl (non-lethal); a nullifier squad in the flooding vault. Primrose learns her Church co-wrote the bullet. *Skip:* six years; Bernal II dies.

**Act 3 — 自由的海湾 A Free Bay Built on Chains.** Port Isobel, Marlliande, Vincenton, Noruin; 1828. *What is a person, and who votes on it?*
1. Election season and a damestear rush; the slave quarter behind the Assembly.
2. Archprovost Undercliffe is a candidate for High Rector and needs the Concord quiet while he sails — or others need him not to sail.
3. Supper at Dainuich's: served after dark, by the one clan that stayed loyal.
4. A field gang of mechanim and a field gang of slaves stop work the same week; the owners file both as faults.
5. The Assembly vote: abolition, compensation, "apprenticeship" plus Cognitive Licences, or nothing.
6. Admiral Erelas Gladeguard's squadron off the Bloodgroves demanding fugitives back; he is older than every colony he blockades.
7. The chamber of four pillars: empty pedestal, humming stone. Middlesday answers it. Primrose checks the code twice.
8. Cloakmen arrive to seal the room and impound "the machine that speaks God's tongue". Then the conclave's result by sending-stone.

*Choices:* the vote (chains ±2, guns ±1); fugitives; the phrase — publish through the Chroniclers as in 1518, trade it to a candidate, sell it, bury it (doors ±1, church rep, the next High Rector: Erudite, Zealot or Vivin placeman). *Fights:* a night hunt on the plantation road; slave-catchers on the quay; Cloakmen among the pillars (special: strike a pillar). *Skip:* nine years. Ricard is sixty and comes back for one more.

**Act 4 — 千灯之海 The Sea of a Thousand Lights.** Síl Berkhen's Land, a Feyrealm sea, the Crumbling City, the Celestial shore; early 1838. *If the house is empty, who is owed the keys?*
1. One ship, five flags, your command, because you are the only person all of them owe. Two ways out: Busilar's Root at Busilar's price, or a Sarhali Spire that a lizardfolk surveyor swears is a door and nobody owns. A Hakhepri pilgrim on the quay keeps staring at General Deshird.
2. The fork against the mast.
3. A fey sea-market: passage costs a name or a memory. Pilot: Álvar, horned now.
4. The Crumbling City: red-cloaked clerks, a queue, a desk labelled "Nerat" with nobody at it. One question to one of the dead.
5. The Celestial shore: orphaned angels camped along a wall.
6. The Gate. Torrieth, 170 years on the step. The Prince-Ascendant, who looks seventeen. The keyhole is the shape of the Fragment's shard.
7. Five flags come to blows on Heaven's doorstep over a boy and a lock.
8. What you carry home: the Prince, a rubbing, testimony, or a black-powder scorch that let a little light out.

*Choices:* Root or Spire (using the Spire ends Busilar's monopoly: doors +2); Deshird's secret (a dragon's debt; Hakhepra's empty throne); the Prince comes, stays or chooses (crowns ±2); test the lock (mana +1, doors +1, wild). *Fights:* the maelstrom; the mutiny; the Gate's warden (`noAwe`).

**Finale — 第二次大峰会 The Second Grand Summit of Aranthíl.** Tearfall 1838; 10 scenes.
Druznar's finding is read aloud: it is not coming back → Emperor Petre dies on the second night with every elector in the building → the Silnara–Vivin wedding, or the bride redirected → the Dove question with a living Silmuna in the next room → the Articles (a hub: black powder, the Doors, mechanim, the hoards; you pass only what your seven reputations can carry) → Ioriel's closing session, if she has the Queen → the Fragment shown in public for the first time, Middlesday reading → the Gate: the Fragment as key, black powder as crowbar, or leave it → last fight by path (Zealot Cloakmen, a dragon's guard, an enthralled hall, the warden) → ending.

## 7. Endings

1. **阿兰蒂尔条约 The Treaty of Aranthíl** *(bittersweet)*. Black powder capped, doors under a five-power commission, the Republic federal, wakers counted as heads. Magic survives thin and rationed; everyone signs and nobody toasts. *Needs:* three reputations ≥3, guns ≤4, Gate left shut.
2. **破晓 Artheta** *(defensible wrong)*. The Republic signs nothing, keeps its cartridges, takes the Root with a dragon's help and shoots its separatists at dawn. No mage-king returns and no slave is sold on its soil, and the leak goes on. *Needs:* dove ≥7, guns ≥7.
3. **月之子 The Child of the Moon.** The Prince is crowned under the Baronites' constitution or as Ascendant; magic becomes a regalian right; Lorent comes in by marriage. *Needs:* Prince home, crowns ≥7.
4. **玫瑰和约 The Rose Peace** *(defensible wrong)*. The Grand Sorcery closes the Summit. Nobody remembers disagreeing; there is no war for thirty years. *Needs:* Ioriel's course, Lisolette lost or overruled.
5. **瓜分诸天 The Empyreal Scramble** *(canon's own course; the fallback)*. No treaty holds. Companies and navies race through whatever doors they can tune; magic has a price list; the wakers come in the 1870s, on schedule.
6. **天门开 The Gate Opens.** By shard or by powder. Magic comes back wild and unowned; what steps through depends on the reading you chose; the Church splits between "He went home" and "there was only ever a door". *Needs:* doors ≥7 and a key.
7. **末次泪落 The Last Tearfall** *(defensible wrong)*. Forks melted, Gate left, the last crystals spent on purpose. A safe, ordinary world with no witch-kings; in a century the mechanim wind down. *Needs:* doors ≤2 by choice.
8. **空座 The Empty Seat** *(divine only; see §10).*

Epilogue cards by track (Esmaria, the Queen, Oswald I, Castonath, the Bay, Hakhepra's throne), by companion, by origin; if the Prince marries a Rubenaire, their son is christened Artur Laurens.

## 9. Risks

- **Mechanim fifty years early.** Canon dates the Awakening to the 1870s–80s. Defuse: the PC and Middlesday are isolated first wakers; the mass event comes only if the player forces it, otherwise an epilogue card dates it as canon does. Still too much? Swap the origin for the Ravelian Rectorate (flag Z97) and keep Middlesday.
- **Things canon keeps open:** the Fragment's nature, Heaven's interior, the Empire-In-Shadow. The phrase has two readings and narration never picks; the camera stops at the threshold; Marion III's theory is testimony from a dead man.
- **No canon page** for the Teleportation Tragedy or the Prince's fate. He is optional: every finale path works with him left on the step.
- **"Nullification opens doors"** is my inference from canon mechanics. It is voiced as the Royal Wizards' and Fractalines' working theory, error bars included.
- **Vampires** (Vivin, Marlliande) are implied in the files, never stated. Narration says "after dark" and "hunters"; the word is the player's to prove.
- **Aakhet** reveals himself only later in canon; keeping his secret is the default.
- **Pinned dates** for crises the mod leaves floating (Brasslance 1822, conclave 1828, Summit 1838) and two invented matches. All fall after the start date.
- **Engine.** Seven endings plus one divine; an Articles hub gated by reputation; a per-origin race lock for the Unowned (fallback: you are the waker, or the hand whose name is on its bill of sale); mechanim cannot cast, so no mechanim wizard; premade classes assume the final roster (bard, sorcerer, ranger, artificer) with rogue/wizard/fighter as fallbacks; ageing is text only.
- **Sprawl.** One crisis on stage per act, a second by telegram, the rest in a newspaper digest at each time skip with a single choice.
- **Slavery.** Named enslaved people with plans of their own; no auction played for colour.
- Dossier 13 (community) did not exist when this was written; tone follows the mod's event text.

**Disagreements.** (a) I spent no origin on a dwarf hold, the Jadd or a gnome: five seats that are all parties to the Summit serve a kingmaker story better; those peoples are race options, patrons and Druznar. (b) "Escann or the Dwarovar" became the League's railway and Escann; the Obsidian war belongs to another pitch. (c) "Beyond the world" stops at Heaven's doorstep. (d) I end in 1838, canon's date for the comet's obituary.

## 10. The divine line (升神作弊：永生、无敌，剧情和选项跟着变)

Built to `CONTENT_GUIDE` §10: everything is additive (`is.god`, `is.immortal`, `{god}`), the player may ascend or lapse at any scene, no plot flag is set only on a divine branch, and each `chapters` entry fills level, companions and flags so that jump-then-ascend works.

**Why it belongs here.** This is a campaign about empty chairs, set in a world where gods are voted in by council and belief is known to feed celestial power. A living god is therefore the one source of magic that is not running out, and every power wants you as its reactor. The Republic was built on not needing mages and now needs you. That is the joke and the cost.

**Rule for god-only options (`tag: '神'`).** They always work and the world always pays: `crowns` rises (people stop voting when someone can simply decide), a hidden `var.cult` grows, and a companion says so.
- *Act 1, Konwell:* stop the third volley in the air. Nobody dies; the square kneels; Parliament debates whether a god meets the property qualification to vote.
- *Act 2, Castonath:* take the Drowning out of Yamina's lungs. The Demesne proclaims a patron; Ancardia mobilises.
- *Act 3, Port Isobel:* strike every chain on the Bay in one morning. chains −3; the planters' militia and Gladeguard's squadron answer; Undercliffe: "You did before breakfast what I meant them to choose."
- *Act 4, the Crumbling City:* overrule a Reaper's ledger and take one of the dead home. The clerks open a file on you, and the dead start writing.
- *Finale:* open the Gate with your hand.

With one bystander reaction, one companion line and the time-skip paragraph, that makes the guide's four reacting scenes per act. The seven mortal endings each carry an `is.god` variant paragraph, so a god who declines the Seat still changes how the Treaty or the Restoration reads.

**Invincible means somebody else loses.** Several fights have a `lose` branch that carries plot (arrest at Konwell, capture by Cloakmen). A PC who cannot fall gets an `is.immortal` route instead: they cannot hold you, so they hold Ricard, or the Assembly, or a city. Mindless line-automata, Reaper clerks, the enthralled hall and the Gate's warden fight with `noAwe`.

**Reactions.** Fractalines want calipers on you and classify you as "a gifted mage" or "an aspect"; the Fragment changes pitch when you enter, and what you hear is not what Middlesday hears. Ioriel, eight centuries of statecraft in a hat, offers Lorent a patron and you a salary. Corinites ask where you stand with Corin. Magnates try to license miracles and insurers rewrite "act of god". Deshird is jealous: he was worshipped first. Someone tries a black cartridge to see whether you bleed; you do not, and `mana` drops by one. If you are of the Unowned, your former owner sues for the return of a god. Companions split: Ricard salutes and stops arguing, which is worse; Primrose takes notes; Lisolette asks whether the Queen may borrow you; Middlesday: "(afraid) Did you make us?"

**Immortal only.** Each time skip has an `is.immortal` paragraph (Ricard's third brass hand, Primrose's spectacles, your unchanged coat). The Vivins' night-time patrons assume you are one of theirs and send a card. Ioriel proposes a partnership measured in centuries.

**Divine ending — 空座 The Empty Seat** (`divine: true`). Just inside the Gate there is a chair. It is the only interior the campaign ever shows, and only to a god. Sit, and the orphaned angels have a founder again: magic is re-sourced from belief in {god}, no longer finite, never again free of you. Or go in and lock it from the inside. Or carry the chair out and leave it in the Old Colosseum for whoever is elected next. A Pantheonic Council meets by sending-stone to vote on you either way.

**Epilogue cards.** `is.god` ×4: the Council's vote count; the cult in the Bilge that sells jam jars you never touched; the Republic's first budget with a line for "miracles, misc."; what Middlesday tells the wakers about who made them. `is.immortal` ×1: Tearfall 1949, the same roof, nobody left who remembers the picnic.
