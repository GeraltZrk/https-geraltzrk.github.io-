# 08 — Cosmology, Gods, Deep History and the Magic System of Halann

Dossier for writers of the three Anbennar campaigns (1022 AA / 1444 AA / 1836 AA). Citations: `[wiki: Page]`, `[eu4: path]`, `[ck3: path]`, `[vic3: path]`, `[zh: file]`. Mod paths are relative to each mod root. Where wiki and mod disagree the mod is followed and the conflict is listed in section 9. Items marked **SECRET TRUTH (S#)** are things most people in-world do not know at one or more of the start dates.

## 1. Overview

Halann's metaphysics is built in layers, and the setting's central trick is that each layer was sincerely believed before the next one was dug up.

1. **Folk belief (all eras):** the Cannorian creation myth — three wandering sibling gods (the Clarionate), a Godswar, dragons as the gods' regents, then mortal kings as regents [wiki: Cannorian Creation Myth; wiki: Regent Court].
2. **Historians:** the pantheon was assembled by eleven Pantheonic Councils out of tribal gods, for reasons of state [wiki: Pantheonic Councils].
3. **1518:** a precursor mural shows *Castellar, God of the Sky* — one-armed, one-legged, sceptre in hand — failing to contain the explosion that destroyed Aelantir in 1 AA. Castellos has been dead for fifteen centuries [eu4: localisation/anb_age_of_unraveling_l_english.yml].
4. **1585–1700:** the God Fragment and the Ravelian claims: One God, magic stolen from Them by the precursor elves, Heaven locked, the land of the dead a mere jurisdiction inside the Shadow Plane [eu4: localisation/anb_events_religion_l_english.yml; wiki: God Fragment].
5. **Late 19th century (the "Universal Magic" hypothesis, so *after* the 1836 start):** "Most if not all gods are false, but merely beings of magical power"; magic is one finite energy; damestear is comet debris and the comet broke in 1810 [wiki: Magic; wiki: Oracle's Eye Comet].

The mod deliberately never confirms the bottom layer. The wiki's own design notes say divine magic "is better described as coming through faith rather than being granted by objectively existing deities", and that the Crimson Deluge "should never be confirmed to be a godswar" [wiki: Magic; wiki: Crimson Deluge]. Writers should keep that ambiguity: every revelation is a *document*, an *artefact* or a *testimony*, never the narrator's voice.

## 2. Facts

### 2.1 The Cannorian creation myth (what "everyone knows")

**Before Times.** The Clarionate siblings — Castellos (eldest), Agrados, Yshtralania (youngest, wisest) — "last of their kind" and "survivors of an ancient conflict", found a world fought over by four primordials: Draxos the Everflame (fire), Aesanna the Windwalker (air), Uelos the Storm-king (water), Halanna the Earth Mother. Halanna begged for help; the opening of the *Book of Creation (Vol I)* is her "clarion call" and the three answers ("To bring the chaos that is your world to order," says Castellos) [wiki: Cannorian Creation Myth].

**Primordial War.** Agrados beat Draxos in single combat, absorbed part of him (fire portfolio) and the rest was "trapped in what we now call the Sun". Castellos absorbed Aesanna (air). Uelos could not be killed; Yshtralania tricked him out of the storm portfolio (given to Castellos), and Halanna's plea spared him. Giants and genies, the primordials' soldiers, were pardoned in exchange for labour [ibid.].

**Creation.** Castellos made the sky "to protect the world from the void of night"; Halanna grew life; Uelos wept the lakes and rivers; Yshtralania was the architect. Castellos and Agrados forged **dragons** (wind for flight, flame for breath) and Yshtralania taught them magic. Castellos made the winged **avariel** as messengers; Halanna made **dwarves** from stone "to help her with the details". Castellos loved Halanna; Yshtralania wed Uelos; Agrados had no one and was posted to "the void at the very edges of the world" [ibid.].

**Godswar.** Agrados returned, was rejected by Halanna and raped her. She died in childbirth; her corpse became "the true foundation of the world". From her womb came the **Spawns of Agrados**: Falah and Nerat (the two not bestial), and the beast-progenitors Arimar (harimari), Grilax (gnolls), Firanya (harpies, first queen of the Harpy Queendom), Vukar (lycanthropes), Siramod (minotaurs), Dolumir (satyrs — he once trapped Falah in the Deepwoods "for 100,000 years"), Rohibos (centaurs), Nazthi "the Unseen God" (deep devils, lizardfolk). Uelos drowned the world in grief and vanished under the sea. Agrados, short of allies, made **humanity** — "the epitome of Agrados' ambition and thirst for war". Castellos won, tried to absorb his brother, was corrupted, and cast the tainted part of himself into the underworld where it became **Kazraiel** [ibid.; wiki: Spawns of Agrados].

**The Godly Decrees** (the charter of the present age): traitor gods imprisoned in the Underworld (the strongest became the Infernal Court); Nerat is jailer and god of the dead; Falah must hunt her bestial siblings; *gods may no longer walk the earth and act only through avatars*; one portfolio, one god; avariel are the gods' messengers; **dragons rule as the gods' regents** [ibid.].

**Inheritance War.** Malliath, first dragon, two-headed, "Godeater", ate Yshtralania (his own teacher). Adean drove lance and steed through one head; Castellos pushed his son clear of the other and lost his **left arm and leg**. Malliath fell and became the Serpentspine Mountains; his heads are the Serpentshead and the Dragon Coast. Stalemate: "The dragons remained superior in earth and sky, and prevented the gods from returning from the heavens ever since." Then the Rule of Dragons: genies imprisoned, giants pushed north, dwarves driven underground, and the wingless descendants of the avariel — the **elves** — enslaved [ibid.].

Classes of god: Clarionate; Excusian (made before the Godswar: The Dame, plus the fallen); Successors (born of the wars: Esmaryal, Adean, Ryala); Ascendants (mortals raised later: Balgar, Ara, Minara, Munas, Nathalyne, Begga, Corin) [wiki: Regent Court; wiki: Cannorian Creation Myth].

### 2.2 The Regent Court (摄政王庭) — who is prayed to, and how

Followers keep one patron but pay respect to all; the court is imagined as a feudal household. The name comes from the doctrine that Castellos lets mortal kings rule "as divine regents on earth in his stead" [wiki: Regent Court]. In 1022 the CK3 mod still calls it the **Cannorian Pantheon**, with regional faiths (Castanorian Pantheon, Court of Adean, Temple of the Highest Moon / Cult of the Dame, House of Minara, Small Temple of the halflings, Southern Cult of Castellos among the Ourdi, the Whiterose Court of Lorent, Iochand's "Court of Masks" where "temples and theatres blend together") [ck3: localization/english/religion/anb_religion_cannorian_pantheon_l_english.yml]. By 1836 the Vic3 mod labels the old faith simply **"Adeanic"** [vic3: localization/english/politics/anb_religion_l_english.yml].

A soldier's summary, from Captain Vincen's diary: "when we need money, we pray to Ara, when we're sick, to Nerat, or to Corin if we need to fight" [wiki: Vincen Godsighter].

### 2.3 **S1** — "CASTELLOS IS DEAD"

Sequence in the EU4 mod [eu4: localisation/anb_age_of_unraveling_l_english.yml]:
- *The Mural of Castellar*: under a precursor temple, a hall of murals of all elven history. The last panel, "in a less refined hand", shows a fireball over a continent and a man descending from the clouds, sceptre in one hand, other arm ending in a stump, "a great silver cloak" hiding a missing leg. Inscriptions: *Castellar, God of the Sky* and *The End*. The discoverer may publish or suppress; if suppressed it leaks to the Order of Chroniclers.
- *The Broken Scepter*: in a ruinborn shrine, a six-foot white stone staff capped with a blue orb, its gem head shattered, carrying a residue of "powerful abjuration magics".
- *CASTELLOS IS DEAD*: Chronicler pamphlets with the mural on the back; "Tellumite Town Criers shout the words from the rooftops"; apprentice mages seize the **Tower of Adráil** and project the mural onto the clouds over the Dameshead. Reactions offered: "the whole world is unraveling" (Regent Court), "This is proof of the Sun Cult's teachings", "A new sovereign reigns - Kazraiel shall rise", "Corin is our goddess".
- *Eleventh Pantheonic Council*: "what kind of funeral do you give a God?" Castellos' own aged Castellyrian priest is "relieved to finally have an answer… why the Silver Dragon did not come again". Dominic of Gallowspeak, past ninety, argues succession by merit for Corin; priests of Esmaryal and Ryala back Adean; those of Nathalyne and Falah waver.

The wiki dates publication to 1518 and ratification to 1520 [wiki: Castellos; wiki: Pantheonic Councils]. **Outcome:** the Council's final conclusion was that Castellos is dead and **Adean succeeds him**; the Corinites had already issued their Proclamation (1519) naming Corin and refused to recognise the ruling. The two-year delay is itself blamed for the schism [wiki: Pantheonic Councils; wiki: Corinite; wiki: Timeline]. (The Regent Court page instead calls the Eleventh Council "abandoned due to disagreement" — see section 9.) A restored Castanor can later bring the body of belief home: a Memorial on Pantheonway whose ceiling shows "the ill-fated containment of Ducaniel's Wrath" [eu4: localisation/Flavour_Castanor_B32_l_english.yml (flavor_castanor.102)].

### 2.4 Corin's apotheosis and Corinite doctrine (科琳教)

Corina, born 23 Bloomsend 1420 near Eswall to a mother believed to be a priestess of Agrados; orphaned by the Greentide in 1424; raised as the "bastard son" and squire of the knight Frederic síl Bennon; "timid outside of battle". She learned she was Agrados' avatar "through a conversation she claimed with Nerat"; was resurrected after the Battle of Rottenstep; in 1443 with the goblin warlock Oubblig and the elf mage Arun Drennen slew **the Eldest** and broke the Verdant Veil (Arun sacrificed himself to give her the opening; Oubblig was flung into the Feyrealm and lost to the party [wiki: Verdant Veil]); died 11 Nerament 1444 in Castonath taking a blow meant for the half-orc Lothane Bluetusk and killing Korgus Dookanson [wiki: Corin (person); wiki: Corin; wiki: The Eldest; wiki: Corinite]. **S18:** Agradosian cults already held a prophecy of their god's redemption and Corin "fulfilled all the steps" [wiki: Corinite].

- **Tenth Council (1445):** not a proper council — "a conclave of adventurer-clerics", led by Dominic of Gallowspeak, "lately of Nerat, now sworn to Corin" [eu4: anb_events_religion (corinite.100)].
- **Corinite Proclamation (1519)** → schism; War of the Elensbridge (1535–1542; ends with imperial princes allowed to choose their realm's faith); the **Crimson Deluge** (c. 1560–1580), rains like blood read as a war in heaven; witch-burnings; the 1572 Anbennarian Crusade (defeated 1575); the Shadowmoon Conspiracy (1573) [wiki: Corinite; wiki: Timeline; eu4: anb_events_religion (corinite.2, deluge.13)].
- **Dated spine of the wars of religion** [wiki: Timeline; wiki: Corinite; wiki: Ravelian Church]: 1490 Aelantir rediscovered (Endral the Explorer) · 1518 Mural published · 1519 Proclamation · 1520 Eleventh Council · 1577 Ravelian Society founded · 1585 God Fragment found · 1620–1645 First Corinite War (Peace of Esmaria: freedom among Cannorian faiths) · 1638 Ravelian Church founded · 1666 Emperor Galien III síl na Trisfer begins persecuting Corinites; Torrieth abdicates · 1670–1692 Galien persecutes the Ravelians ("Heresies of Reason") · 1675–1684 Second Corinite War (Adeanic victory; Corinites barred from the Dove Throne) · 1687–1689 Camir Silmuna unifies Escann · 1690–1704 War of Camiran Conquest (Camir takes the Dove Throne and kills Galien III in 1692) · 1730 Camir dies; 1730–1735 War of Camiran Succession; his son Lothane IV Silmuna institutes a new Mages' Peace · 1810 Blackpowder Rebellion · 1820 Álvar sil Armon's expedition opens the "Empyreal Age".
- **Doctrine:** righteous *reasons* for war matter more than righteous conduct; vanquish evil rather than merely defend good; henotheism; a **divine right to revolt** (a fallen ruler has lost Corin's favour) [wiki: Corinite; wiki: Divine Regency]. The **Sword and Shield**: strike evil and protect what you love in one motion; accusing someone of favouring one over the other is a serious charge.
- **Scripture:** *The Gallowspeak Writings*; *The Deliverances of Oakbreaker* (Arosha Oakbreaker; orcish, distinguishes justified crusade from Dookanson's Greentide); *The Memoirs of Lothane Bluetusk*; *The Recollections*; *Winston Foolfoot's Poems and Songs* (hymns "The Battle of Castonath", "To be in Her Presence", "The Armed Woman"); *Corin's Mandate* (absolutism; cover for Witch-Kings); Corinsfield's *Blessed Book* (rejects all magic, claims the Dame was corrupted by the Infernal Court).
- **Structure:** state churches. Ranks named for armour: **Helm**, **Bevor**, **Greave**, **Couter**.
- **Orders:** Monks of Unsanctioned Charity, New Moon Philosophers (later defected to the Ravelians), the Dowagers, Aranite Communalists, Order of the Orchard, Knights of Moonfire, the Cerisian Sisterhood, Dragonfire Balgarites, Corinite Firebrands, House of Light, the Corintar Order.

### 2.5 The God Fragment (神之碎片) and the Ravelian revelation (揭秘教)

**Discovery (S5).** After the mural, explorers follow a "diamond-like symbol" shown where freed elves "clasp hands around a central source of power". The elven archaeologist Torrieth absorbs a "place of power" runestone, hears "the voices of the precursors", and in 1585 leads Captain Vincen's expedition in Noruin to a hatch and a chamber "propped up by four large trunk-like pillars of a design predating the empire". Above a pedestal floats a fist-sized diamond shape shining "the teal-blue of damestear-infused magical energy", emitting "a high-pitched garbling, as if someone speaking a strange tongue with incredible speed"; it flickers between blue and "pure darkness". Everyone kneels but Torrieth: "We have not found the Birthplace of the Empire. We may have found the Birthplace of Magic itself." In the other branch she steals it [eu4: localisation/anb_precursor_fragment_l_english.yml (fragments.4–406); eu4: anb_events_religion (ravelian.7)]. Later lens-work shows "a fluctuating black, gem-like cubic shard in the center" [wiki: God Fragment; vic3: localization/english/anb_concepts_l_english.yml].

**Claims drawn from it:** it houses "the last remaining essence of a god that preceded Precursors", source of all magic in the Prime Material; that God "allowed his defeat" at elven hands (cited in-world as *Provenance 1:23-30*); all other gods are aspects of Them [wiki: God Fragment]. "Original Sin": the elves "had been impatient and stole magic from God, instead of waiting for God to bestow magic upon them when the time was right as They did with dragons"; artificery is the remedy that returns the gift to everyone [eu4: anb_events_religion (ravelian.11.dd)].

**Counter-evidence writers can use:** it predates the Precursor Empire; the translation code existed before the Church did and "could be automated" (L. B. Stamh, *Of Gods and Gears*); the Church refuses access; critics say the High Rector interprets to suit politics; rival theories call it "coalesced magic of the Ruin" or a Rule-of-Dragons artefact. Its location is secret — "theorized… the depths of Aranthíl" [wiki: God Fragment]. The wiki's design note on theurges says the Ravelians are "doing their own version" of the precursors' theft [wiki: Ravelian Theurge].

**Church history.** Founded 1638 by Torrieth; popular from 1640. Four Debates between **Erudites** and **Zealots**: (1) salvation — by good works or by understanding; (2) conversion — Inquisition or discourse; (3) saints and aspects; (4) artificery — for the masses or sanctified. The wiki gives one historical outcome (Zealots, Erudites, Zealots, Zealots); the mod lets the player's Veridicals vote [wiki: Ravelian Church; eu4: anb_events_religion (ravelian.11–15)]. **S6, the First Debate's finding:** celestials contacted by planar binding "are just as lost as we are"; every celestial tradition says "their founder or creator is dead" and remembers an ancestral homeland; "Heaven exists, but its gates are closed and its location forgotten with the near-death of the One God" (note: *near*-death — the Ravelian God is not said to be fully dead); the dead are judged "not by Nerat, but by (red-cloaked) celestial angels of death"; celestial and infernal power "ebb and flow due to belief". In 1666 Torrieth abdicates on confirming Heaven is locked and vanishes; "a celestial had last seen her in the Celestial Plane". She is succeeded as High Rector by "a halfling Vernman" (unnamed). During the Fourth Debate the Church also claimed the Fragment itself "was created by the precursor elves destroying God" [wiki: Ravelian Church; wiki: Torrieth Godspeaker; wiki: Magic].

Organisation: High Rector; College of Veridicals; Archprovosts, Provosts, Rectors; **Cloakmen** (cloak over the left shoulder) and the Orda Aldresia as the military arm; the **Fractalines** ("Supplicant Order of Fractal Measure"), monk-mages who cannot be priests, some of whom survive a warlock pact with the Fragment and become **theurges** — "most would-be theurges die during the process". Saints include Aldresia, Corin, Auci Dameris, Minara, Orbin (gnome philosopher). Surael is classed as an "aspect"; whether Agrados or the Malevolent Dark can be aspects is an open quarrel [wiki: Ravelian Church; eu4: anb_events_religion (ravelian.11.dc, .600, .603)].

### 2.6 Deep history: dragons, giants, precursors, and the Ruin

**Chronology** (caution: on the Precursor Empire page only the *Technology* section is marked "new canon"; the dated history list below sits under the banner "Everything below is notes and not to be taken as canon!!!". - 8000 BA Age of Light; 7700 BA "Usurpation of the Golden Scales" against the lizardfolk; c. 7800–7450 BA precursors enter Haless and build the High Temple network; 6229 BA Emperor Arethon dies, his son **Aldan** succeeds; 6200 BA the **Call for Reflection** recalls all elves; Aldan's brother **Ducaniel** stays to fight the dwarves; **c. 6000 BA the orcs are released** [wiki: Precursor Empire; wiki: High Temples].
- **War of Two**, ending with Ducaniel imprisoned in the flying capital **Aesadas** [wiki: Day of Ashen Skies; wiki: War of Two].
- **1 Castanmark, 1 AA — Day of Ashen Skies.**

**Precursor style (design rule):** "a purely magic society… NOT magitek, NOT artifice, NOT sci-fi". Mages power everything personally; no guns; an artisan civilisation of immortals where every object is bespoke; flying cities — the capital Aesadas (spelled "Aesades" in the wiki's deprecated section; "Aesadas" on the Day of Ashen Skies page and in the mod) plus Lanaiande, Affelai, Tacenie, Harathil and Dalaire (the full list of five is from the wiki's deprecated section; in the EU4 localisation Aesadas, Tacenie and Lanaiande occur in country flavour files such as Flavour_TaychendiEmpire_J84 — where Tacenie is brought down "as if Aesadas had fallen once more" — Affelai only in the fragment quest, Dalaire widely as a region name, and Harathil only in Vic3) [wiki: Precursor Empire; wiki: Day of Ashen Skies; eu4: localisation/anb_precursor_fragment_l_english.yml (fragments.7); eu4: localisation/Flavour_TaychendiEmpire_J84_l_english.yml].

**S2 — the cause of the Ruin.** Imprisoned, Ducaniel "began a dark ritual… if he were not to rule Aelantir, no-one would". His followers crashed Aesadas "killing millions on impact"; the souls fed the blast called **Ducaniel's Wrath**, which carved the Ruined Sea and the Cliffs of Ruin, split the continent, and tore "the fabrics of reality" [wiki: Day of Ashen Skies]. The Elfenbride quest gives the motive: the Elfenbride, Ducaniel's niece (the wiki's deprecated text makes her Emperor Aldan's only child), "a woman (or being) of immense power that she could break worlds"; her son **Rinetrin** was "born of brother and niece. Of destroyer and creator"; "it was their forced union that caused the war that ended Aelantir". Her manse in Old Rzentur is a preserved garden-palace; "The Elfenbride was no longer here" [eu4: anb_precursor_fragment (fragments.3, .303, .306)]. Castellar's part is in 2.3.

**Aftermath:** magic became far easier to harness; the Century of Ash; the Great Floods; **the Dissipation** (all genies banished from the material plane); the harimari ascension; the Verdant Veil; the death of the Serpent-King. Names: Ruin of Aelantir (elves), Godloss (hobgoblins), "Day when the Heavens were Sundered by the Dark" (Bulwari) [wiki: Day of Ashen Skies; ck3: localization/english/anb_game_concepts_l_english.yml].

### 2.7 Planes

Regent Court model [wiki: Planes of Existence; wiki: Cosmology; vic3: anb_concepts]:
- **Prime Material** — Halann, and the whole Astral of space.
- **Feyrealm** — the parallel plane drowned in positive/life energy. Time and physics differ. Once tied to Escann by the **World Tree** until **Castan Beastbane** burned it. On the Day of Ashen Skies the archfey **the Eldest** made the **Verdant Veil** "out of his own essence" over the Deepwoods and Eordand's Domandrod, becoming "a feral husk" that held it until Corin's Circle killed it in 1443 (**S16**). (The Verdant Veil page, citing Rothomar the Wise, dates the Veil's creation to "a few days after" the Day; "the Eldest" is only Arun Drennen's coinage, and Arun first found it in 1429, losing his apprentice Eledas Cenág.) Time ran differently inside: the Moon Elf troops and mages who crossed in 1017 were "trapped for a thousand years" by their own count before 1443 [wiki: Deepwoods Druidism]. Travellers keep to paths called **Vyrs**; stable crossings are **Gladeways**; you may come out "in Yanshen" or "a hundred years in the past" [wiki: Feyrealm; wiki: Verdant Veil; wiki: The Eldest].
- **Shadow Plane** — the parallel plane drowned in negative/death energy; "permanent state of dying but not death". A **soul trap**. Four outsider powers contend there: **shadow liches**, **night hags**, **Red Reapers**, **fiends**. Liches want the status quo; night hags want the Shadow to swallow everything. Ravelian exploration: the "Fugue Plane" is the **Fugue Demesne**, centred on the Crumbling City, where the Red Reapers "(and Nerat, if he ever existed)" hold authority (**S6**) [wiki: Shadow Plane; wiki: Nerat].
- **Outer planes:** Celestial, Infernal (devils of the Infernal Court; demons such as the gnolls' **Xhazob**), and the four Elemental planes, home of genies.
- **The Empyreal / "Plane of a Thousand Lights"** — a late-19th-century Ravelian concept: "the cosmic glue between any and all planes", "a plane of metaphysical memes"; "God is either in the Empyreal… or God IS the Empyreal". The Halessi **Spirit Realm** is a region of it [wiki: Cosmology; wiki: Spirit Realm].
- **Eldritch:** **the Mosaic** ("Plane of Madness, the Far Astral, the Missing Meridian"), home of the Dark Descendants; the **Plane of Flesh** [wiki: Planes of Existence].
- **Astral Tree** theory: thin places are "branches" — the World Tree (fey), the Shadowroot Matriarch *Yezelne Nisha* in the Shadow Swamp of Yezel Mora, the Root of the Universe [wiki: Astral Tree].
- **S14 — soul traps.** Bulwari souls go to the **Silent Garden**, the Shadow-Plane mirror of Bulwar ruled by the mute God-king **Amussu**, where apathetic shades barter information for sunlight and sunstones. Halessi souls are ground down and recycled in the Spirit Realm. A 19th-century rumour, the **Empire-In-Shadow**, is said to rule from the **City-In-Shadow**, a tomb-planet alive only in the Shadow Plane; the last pre-Blackpowder emperor Marion III Silmuna groomed his son "Prince-Ascendant Marion" to unite the world against it [wiki: Silent Garden; wiki: Soul; wiki: The City-In-Shadow; wiki: Empire-In-Shadow; wiki: Divine Regency].

**Fey.** Courts: **Seelie** (the Fey Queen, Lord of Beasts, Dolumir, the Fused Monarchs, the Prince of Fools), **Unseelie** (the King of Darkness, the Herald of Change, the Trader of Masks and Faces — "obsessed with… not being forgotten"), the four **Seasonal Courts** of Eordand (the Everfrost Prince, the Amber Princess, the Gloam Knight, the Drag of the Waves, the Unthroned Queen, the Old Man of the Archives), and unaligned archfey (the Blackened Prince, Lord Blossom, the Emerald Lady, Lorest). "The Accord": no archfey may act directly against another [wiki: Fey; wiki: Archfey]. Wood elves' **Fey Court** is etiquette, not worship; **Pactseeker** goblins sell service to archfey [wiki: Fey Court; vic3: politics/anb_religion]. **Hags** are fey: green (illusionists; the Oldhaven Coven), annis (child-eaters, lead giantkin), bheur (winter, Far Gerudia), night hags (centuries-long schemes "to uplift an empire… just to end it all") [wiki: Hags].

**Genies** (djinn/air, marid, shaytan, efreet, silat, orisha): immortal, unkillable, only bound or banished; their native magic is **the Wish**. The Šedu djinn ruled Bulwar until **Brasan the Emancipator** — himself a genie — taught **genie-binding** (c. 2800 BA) to the **Ilumagušar**, the first **God-kings** (traditionally sixteen, "widely believed" to be more; the age runs to 1547 BA). **Wishblades** (*Menumšar*), of bronze-like Šedharis, were made by the Šedu to reshape reality without "Enervation" — in practice they still trigger it, only less than an equivalent Wish; an estimated fifty to a hundred are in Bulwar. **Planetouched** flare up at random near genie relics [wiki: Genie; wiki: God-kings; wiki: Genie-binding; wiki: Wishblade; wiki: Planetouched].

### 2.8 Dragons

Shapeshifters; reproduce alone; live to about 3,000 years (hatchling 0–5, young 6–100, adult 101–1000, ancient 1001–3000); extend life by **drakeslumber**, **ascendancy** or **lichdom**. Draconic blood, via long elven enslavement, is a common source of the mage's spark [wiki: Dragon; wiki: Magic].

**The Dragonwake (470–500 AA) — S8.** **Balris the Gold**, an ascendant in Haless ("He Who Will Return"), cast a spell to find his kin; it caused the **Waking Sickness**, forcing every sleeping dragon into feral dragon form [wiki: Dragonwake; wiki: Balris the Gold]. Roll:
- **Nimrith the Red** — burned Khugdihr, the Redfort, Damescrown, Damenath; hoarded in Khugdihr; **alive in 1022**, slain 1103; cult the Nimrithan [wiki: Dragonwake; ck3: localization/english/religion/anb_religion_dragon_cult_l_english.yml].
- **Zaamalot the Black** — woke under Castonath; razed Bal Mire, Castonath and the citadels; killed in 500 by the Silver Dragon and Castan Dragonfriend. Cult the Za'am. Spawn: **Vazmaar the Vain** and **Malazmur the Magescour**, the only known dracolich (lich since the late 1300s), who raids mage towers [wiki: Zaamalot the Black; wiki: Malazmur the Magescour; wiki: Lich].
- **Tayekan the Blue** — lived disguised among Dragon Coast kobolds using them as an experiment; his waking triggered the Kobold Rising; "presumed alive", possibly heading an organisation [wiki: Tayekan the Blue].
- **Iothoral the Green** (Deepwoods; her sleep shapes the Dragondream), **Elkaesal the White** (Gerudia's Era of Frost, 470–491; sung asleep by the One-armed Skald), **Alos the Copper** (slain by Elecast Dragonsbane with Calindal — or secretly the One-armed Skald), **Kuršagal, the Sand Demon of the Salahad** (largest known; the Mašnsih call him *Abi*, Grandfather), **Hunter of the Deep**, **Jyntas the Brass**, **Zenidir Zentirizar "the Shadowslayer"** (d. 936 killing Eztarthul the Astral Terror) [wiki: Dragonwake; wiki: Sand Demon of the Salahad].

### 2.9 Undeath, curses and other monsters

- **Liches.** Soul in a phylactery; dulled feeling; must absorb souls or go mad; starved ones decay into **demiliches** (a skull with gem-trap eyes). **Shadow liches** bind the soul to the Yezel Mora instead. Known: Morgurax the First; Shadow Lich **Kaino** (first seen 1840 BA; "desires the extinction of all peoples to end… suffering"); Lich King **Xamarzu** of Milcorissia; **Canrec the Defiler**; Rewan IV Eilísin; the goblin Dak Chaingrasper; Malazmur. The mod's lichdom project lists nine steps (given in Old English): Begin the Crime; Craft the Phylactery; Engrave it with the Spell; Sing the Spell; Obtain a Deadly Poison; Mix with a Strong Acid; Create the Potion Vessel; Set apart a Ritual Space; Cleanse the Self — 20–30 years [wiki: Lich; eu4: localisation/anb_magic_projects_l_english.yml].
- **Nichmer the Sorcerer-King** — Korbarid advisor, a renegade of the wereraven **Order of the Ravenseers**; enthralled Emperor Castan; could "enchant" other mages' spells; styled Sorcerer-King 1015 after the Battle of Balmire, when Castan's enthralment broke and Nichmer killed him; slain on Trialmount in 1020 by **Clarimonde of Oldhaven**, a young squire of Caylen Longlance, wielding the rediscovered blade **Calindal** — the first time Trialmount was climbed "by a group of heroes rather than a Castan-hopeful" (1020 on the Timeline and in CK3; the Calindal page says 1021) [wiki: Nichmer, the Sorcerer-King; wiki: Order of the Ravenseers; wiki: Timeline; wiki: Calindal, the Gleaming Blade; ck3: anb_game_concepts].
- **White Pestilence (1040–1053)** — Canrec's undead plague; first case 3 Castanmark 1040 at Ravenhill ("rising from the open grave and devouring the priest of Nerat"); victims' souls bound to the *Book of Canrec*; cure by Garion the Wise and the Ravenseers at Varlosen; ended by the squire **Aldresia**'s crusade [wiki: White Pestilence; wiki: Aldresian Crusade].
- **Vampires (S10).** Either Roilsardi crusader-lords cursed after desecrating a temple of Sur/Surael in Corvuria (the "Errant-Princes"), or the line of **Ettalinde the Wretched** (a former acolyte whose tomb lay under that temple). Context: after the War of the Sorcerer-King Corvuria was a sun-elf-ruled protectorate of Jaher's Phoenix Empire; after the Aldresian Crusade Jaher named the Roilsardi knight **Lucian síl Vivin** "Protector of Corvuria" (the first non-elf), pogroms against the elves followed, and it was Lucian who later disbanded the Ravenseers. The EU4 mod models a vampire estate and a "Masquerade" disaster [wiki: Vampire; eu4: localisation/anb_estate_vampires_l_english.yml].
- **Werewolves** — a fey curse from the Herald of Change; the Greatwoods; hunted by the Woodsmen. **Wereravens** — voluntary (by pecking an eye); carry the **Unkind Prophecy**, visions of a cataclysmic "End Times" (**S13**) [wiki: Werewolf; wiki: Wereraven; wiki: Unkind Prophecy].

### 2.10 The magic system

**The spark.** You have it or you do not; study cannot create it. Sources: bloodline (draconic via elven ancestry is commonest), imbuement, will, or a warlock pact. **Blueblood Theory**: strong mages' blood shows blue under a detecting spell. Mages live about a decade longer and are less fertile [wiki: Magic].

**The eight schools**, fixed by **Garion the Wise** and his **Wise Council**: Abjuration (Serondal Magehand), Conjuration, Divination (Masilda the Diviner), Enchantment (Alarian Singkeep), Evocation (Korvin Lorentis), Illusion (the gnome twins Melba and Melbo "the Mirror"), Necromancy (Alaria the Lifehand), Transmutation (Garion) [wiki: Magic; wiki: Wise Council]. The EU4 mod ranks each school **Novice → Proficient → Renowned → Legendary**; resources are **Mana**, **Magic Experience**, **Magical Infamy** (Paragon / Respected / Suspicious / Denounced / Witch King) and an optional warlock **Patron** [eu4: localisation/anb_magic_system_l_english.yml].

EU4 spell list [eu4: localisation/anb_magic_spells_l_english.yml]:
- *Abjuration:* Combat Ward, Ward, Protected Journey, Mass Ward, Mage Armor, Field of Forbiddance.
- *Conjuration:* Summon Familiars, Summon Animals, Conjure Supplies, Summon Elementals, Aid Construction, Extraplanar Contact.
- *Divination:* Guidance, Eye for Talent, Deposit Divination, Scry, Manipulated Fortune, Foresight.
- *Enchantment:* Enchanting Envoy, Heartstring, Command Animals, Thoughtweave, Enchanting Embassy, Dominate to Surrender.
- *Evocation:* Fireball, Shock and Awe, Flaming Munitions, Meteor Swarm, Tearfall, Elemental Fury.
- *Illusion:* Invisibility, Fear and Loathing, Bread and Circuses, Assimilation Program, Shadows in the Night, Lead the Crowds.
- *Necromancy:* False Life, Steal Vitality, Contagion, Speak With Dead, Remove Pain, Chronophage.
- *Transmutation:* Longstrider, Plant Growth, Mass Enlarge, Transmute to Gold, Reshape Terrain, Rite of Conception.

Hard limits: **no healing spell** (cauterise, numb, divine the surgery, set bones); Alaria Lifehand's instant healing died with her at Trialmount and her "School of Life" became "Necromancy". **No portals.** Planar travel only through thin places. Old buildings are harder to ward: "All things have a history, and magic remembers" (Royal Wizard Alia) [wiki: Magic].

**Other traditions:** divine magic (works spell by spell); dwarven **rune magic** (attributed to the god Urisazirn Mithrilhand; most runesmiths are not mages); bardic magic (the Skaldskolan; Seinathíl); harpy song magic **Ištarun**; druidism (Deepwoods circles descended from mages trapped beyond the Veil in 1017); Bulwari *Magušim* (long legalistic spells, inherited from Wish syntax); Kheteratan state magic under Elikhet's decree ("Let those who were justly granted the gift of magic from birth be regarded as holy, yet all those who would steal this gift must be regarded as the worst of sinners"; Tower of Muhaqaar); **chi** in Haless [wiki: Magic; wiki: Runic Magic; wiki: Dwarven Pantheon; wiki: Deepwoods Druidism; wiki: Magic in Bulwar; wiki: Khetism; wiki: Chi].

**Training and regulation.**
- 1000–1020: the elves land with their tomes; Garion teaches humans during the War of the Sorcerer-King.
- **Mages' Peace (1221) — S11:** court mages conspire to end the Interregnum; the result is the Empire of Anbennar under a Mage Emperor; after the Wars of Rule (1244–1284) emperors are nobles who must be able to cast [wiki: Mages' Peace].
- **The Magisterium** (infobox: est. 1284, end of the Wars of Rule, though "formed secretly during the Interregnum"; the Mages' Peace page says it "was founded alongside the Empire of Anbennar", i.e. 1221; infobox capital Old Damenath; its capital tower is **Adráil** on an artificial isle; **Aranthíl** was the seat until the Wars of Rule — and is later the Ravelian Church's base): licenses syllabuses; by law sends a magister to every noble house as advisor, tutor and court mage; divided into "towers" (Adráil, Aranthíl, Oldtower, the Black Tower of Vertesk, Windtower, the wilful Konwell, Varlosen; Lorincrag; Jurith); Grand Magister elected by the eight school heads; its **Magister-Militant** soldiers are bound by geas. Rival: the mage-knights of the **Orda Aldresia**. Schools: the Imperial Academy, the Silmuna School of the Arts, the Konwell Institute [wiki: Magisterium; wiki: Cannorian Magical Education].
- **Witch-King** (巫师王): a mage who rules or works outside Magisterium law. Named examples (all from the wiki table; the EU4 events magic_ruler.66/.71/.72 are generic "Rise of a Witch-King/-Prince" texts and name nobody): Laurens síl Place of Covenblad ("the First Witch-King of Escann", listed for the 1400s), Varina Escer of Esthíl, the Regent of Wyvernheart (1600s), Camir I Silmuna (table: 1687–1730 — his rule from the unification of Escann; Emperor of Anbennar only from 1692), Nichmer (1015–1020), Hašahu the Serpent-king (136 BA–1), Immarel Winterswrath of Gemradcurt (1430s–1461) [wiki: Witch-King; wiki: Timeline].
- **Floodborn** (洪裔): Escanni mages born under the Crimson Deluge, enormous raw power if they survive **the Drowning** [wiki: Floodborn]. **Royal Wizards** (王家巫师): eight seats, one master one apprentice [wiki: Royal Wizards]. **Camiran Doctrine**: barriers cast only at the instant and direction of impact; the nightly **Covenant of Command** linking officer and troops [wiki: Camiran Doctrine].

**Damestear.** A meteoric crystal (pallasite with meteoric silver) named for the Dame: stores, emits and conducts magic; glows teal-blue, white when saturated, dull turquoise when spent; explodes if overfilled; brittle. Powdered in water it is a foul-tasting **mana potion**; highly addictive to mages. Non-mages who handle it get **Blue Lung**. Forms: alloys (precursor steel, arcane copper), aerated (Feyrealm), prismatic (wild magic, Amadia), organic "Shegibey". Regional names: Sahabaš (Bulwar), Azhalngiv "Halanna's gift" (dwarves), Matremit "mother's tear" (Kheterata), Tamphora "soul stone" (Haless), Sidmherd (Eordand), Akesarak "sky blood" (Taychend), Uzgalkas (Ynn). The Magisterium's 1576 **Skyfall** ritual pulled down the "Celestial Damestear" at Adráil [wiki: Damestear].
**S9 — origin.** It falls in the tenth month, **Tearfall**, from the **Oracle's Eye Comet**, period 111 years (922, 1033, 1144, 1255, 1366, 1477, 1588, 1699, **1810**). In 1810 it broke up over Halann: the greatest damestear fall on record, minutes of wild magic in which "ancient wards momentarily broke", the Tearfall Teleportation Tragedy that destroyed Anbenncóst's Imperial Palace and set off the **Blackpowder Rebellion**. Confirmed by astronomers in 1838. Fear: "without it magic as a whole would dwindle and die out". Conspiracy theories: the comet is the corpse of a dead god (Castellos or Yshtralania) or an astral dragon shedding scales [wiki: Oracle's Eye Comet].
**Black damestear (Korashi).** Made by the Oni by draining a crystal's mana into the Spirit Plane; pierces wards, and in the blood stops spellcasting for hours to days. The Command used it to enslave mages; in the Blackpowder Rebellion Ravelians and Blackpowder Barons mass-nullified confiscated imperial stocks into bullets. Late theory: such use "is slowly reducing the magic across the cosmos" [wiki: Damestear; wiki: Magic; eu4: anb_magic_ruler (magic_ruler.111)].

**Artificery** (奇械术). Began with the gnomes of Nimscodd trying to retake the Dragon Coast. Tests for "artifice": reproducible from a blueprint, mass-producible, usable by non-mages, standard components. Mages and artificers are rival estates [wiki: Artificery; wiki: Damestear; eu4: localisation/anb_estate_mages_l_english.yml]. **Halanna's Breath** (*Frudrnarmr*) is the magical flow from the Primeval Serpentdepths that lets things grow underground [wiki: Halanna's Breath].

### 2.11 Other faiths, briefly

- **Elven Forebears** (精灵祖先): arose on the thousand-year Great Wander when the elves decided their gods had abandoned them; each elf attunes to an heirloom; the cremated dead are made into **ash diamonds**. Sun elves kept the **Sundancer Paragons**. In CK3 the abandoned gods are simply "The Old Gods" [wiki: Elven Forebears; ck3: localization/english/religion/anb_religion_elven_l_english.yml].
- **Dwarven Pantheon** (矮人诸神) vs **Ancestor Worship** (祖先崇拜): the pantheon is headed by **Dagrin Everbeard**, with Anvelind Hearthmaiden, Azmirethazin Forgetender, Durvorazir, Margurz the Brewmaster, Miradeth Earthseeker, Urisazirn Mithrilhand, the twins Lorgrim and Lorgram, and Grobilazk the Twisted (maker of goblins). After the orc invasion most holds dropped the gods for Great Ancestors (Dagrin, Gimron Lawgiver, Hehogrim the Diligent, Balgar, Krummul Orcslayer, Mellegar the Inventor, Urist the Pioneer, Quila the Harvester, Murri Giantbreaker). **Runefather Worship** (符文之父崇拜): the obsidian dwarves of Gor Dûrgheled, exiled for blood-rune magic, whose Nameless King survives as a husk on the Obsidian Throne. **S12:** dwarven myth names three **Travellers** — Setzovar the High, Joztralanna the Mage (who "came to reside on the moon"), Gurthbael the Warrior — "three powerful living siege engines used by the Madness of the Void", purified by the Primordials [wiki: Dwarven Pantheon; wiki: Dwarven Ancestor Worship; wiki: Runefather Worship].
- **Old Dookan / Great Dookan**: Dookan the Chained God, held in "Dookan's Cage" (dwarven "Heart of Doom") where the material plane meets the infernal. Old Dookan keeps "the original tale": he was imprisoned by "the treacherous dwarves for wanting a home", and it **denies Korgus Dookanson's claim** to be Dookan's heir. Great Dookan adds that he fought for Castellos in the Godswar (his original portfolio: mercenaries) and was betrayed and caged by the Cannorian *and* dwarven gods, that Korgus Dookanson is his son and champion, and that orcish conquest will bring "Dookan's Wake". Both agree Dookan shed part of his divinity to make the orcs from his own flesh. **Bulgu Orazan** rejects Dookan for the "Songs of Wise Men" [wiki: Great Dookan; eu4: anb_religions; vic3: politics/anb_religion].
- **Bulwari Sun Cult** (*Sar-Arukanaš*): all gods are dead except **Surael/Surakel**, who became the Sun after the **Malevolent Dark** murdered the moon-goddess Kuza and killed or corrupted the pantheon in the **Thirteen Days of Darkness** (dated 8130 BA). Bulwar is *Kirimahsur*, Surael's Garden. Six Universal Tenets. Royal formula: "Make things function as they should." Chief priests are **Kašra**. The thirteen pure dead gods are the Ešeral Mitellu; the four corrupted are the Erbuturgal. **Surael Reborn** — a hero holding "a great shard of Light", not the god's power — has been King Aga, Barid szel-Sur and **Jaher** (d. 1127); Bulwari scholars think the Cannorian "avatar" idea was borrowed from it during the Castanite Exodus. **Old Sun Cult** rejects Jaher; **New Sun Cult** makes sun elves the Chosen; **the Jadd** of Jaddar Jexiszuir (1161–1563): "Be the Light; Fight the War on Halann; All creatures can be the Light". Secret sect: the **Cult of the Serpent** [wiki: Bulwari Sun Cult; wiki: The Jadd; wiki: Jaddar; ck3: anb_game_concepts; ck3: localization/english/religion/anb_religion_bulwari_sun_cults_l_english.yml].
- **Khetism**: the cat-headed **Khet**, descendants of **Elikhet** (a Bulwari river god who "abandoned his Divinity", died 666 BA, "returned to life" on the Day of Ashen Skies); law is the **Mariat**; ruled by the Khetarch. Sects: Elikhetist, Nirakhetist, Aakhetist, Dalcabba [wiki: Khetism; wiki: Elikhet; eu4: anb_religions].
- **Raheni High Philosophy** (*Darsanasara*, 高等哲学): emulate, not worship, the High Gods (Anjita, Digatha, Dipar, Gunjit, Jaghut, Nermali, Pathim, Simral, Vachava, Vadhan); debate is sacred [wiki: High Philosophy; wiki: High Gods]. **Mystic Accord** (密契): people and spirits as equals under the Great Spirits. **Righteous Path**: folk chi-cultivation; "sleeping indoors and proper burials" retain chi. **Godlost** (弃神者): hobgoblins who abandoned gods after the "Godloss" and suppress or enslave mages [wiki: Mystic Accord; wiki: Righteous Path; wiki: Godlost].
- **Ynn River Worship** (洇河崇拜): the Ending Flood; the dead on barges; Venaan's Tears (*Padhbohvi*) is the gate of the afterlife. **Eordellon** (四季庭): four season-sects serving the Domandrod fey. **Taychendi Hero Worship**: anyone becomes a god by legendary deeds; the rest reincarnate. **Oren Nayiru**: abolitionist millenarians [wiki: Ynn River Worship; wiki: Venaan's Tears; wiki: Eordellon Season Worship; wiki: Taychendi Hero Worship; eu4: anb_religions].
- **Skaldhyrric Faith**: skald-priests of Skaldol recite the *Old Winter Lullaby* to keep Elkaesal asleep; the **Cult of Elkaesal** hunts skalds to end the song. **The Thought**: gnomish rationalism. **Xhazobkult** / **Kvangahga** among gnolls. **Korbarid Dragon Cult** (*Balakurdinţă*) [wiki: Skaldhyrric Faith; ck3: anb_religion_dragon_cult; wiki: The Thought; eu4: anb_religions].

### 2.12 The three start dates at a glance

- **1022 AA.** Two years after Trialmount (CK3 start 1 Castanmark 1022). Castellos reigns unquestioned. The "kingly avatars" doctrine is *not yet* established: the wiki only places it "after the War of the Sorcerer-King and the rise of a more unified Regent Court" and credits it with helping to end the Interregnum (1221), so in 1022 it is at most a new idea in the air [unverified that it exists this early]. Munas' deification is either seven years old (1015) or happening this very year (the Timeline dates the Seventh Council to 1022, and the Avatar page has Caylen Longlance confirmed "1022, via the Seventh") — CK3's faith texts already treat him as a god; a campaign can stage the Seventh Council in 1022, with Alaria Lifehand's rejected avatarhood on the agenda. Auci Eightborn died last year (3 Tearfall 1021); no Nathalyne, Begga or Corin. No Magisterium — the Wise Council's survivors (Garion, Masilda, Melba; Korvin, Melbo, Serondal and Alaria died at Trialmount) are inventing the schools. Canrec is hiding and brewing the Pestilence (1040). Nimrith sits on Khugdihr's gold. The Ravenseers still exist. Jaher lives. The Eldest still holds the Veil. Comet due 1033.
- **1444 AA.** Corin died today (11 Nerament). The Veil broke last year. Tenth Council next year. Aakhet revealed himself in 1439. Nobody knows Castellos is dead. Comet due 1477.
- **1836 AA.** Castellos' death is schoolbook history; Ravelian is the default religion of Blackpowder Anbennar (a parliamentary republic with freedom of conscience in the Vic3 setup), while Corinite and "Adeanic" states persist elsewhere — Ravelianism is ascendant, not universal [vic3: common/country_definitions/anb_countries.txt; vic3: common/history/countries/A01 - Anbennar.txt]; the Ravelian Church teaches that Heaven is locked; Torrieth has been missing 170 years; the Rending is two generations past; the comet broke 26 years ago and its confirmation is two years away; black-damestear bullets won the Blackpowder Rebellion and are spreading (the wiki says they became "standard across modern armies" only "by the late 19th century"); the Magisterium has been absorbed into the Blackpowder state. The "Universal Magic" hypothesis and the Empyreal are *late*-19th-century ideas — in 1836 they are fringe or not yet coined.

## 4. Nations & Factions (playable sides, wants, hatreds)

- **Regent Court / Adeanic temples** — autonomous temples per god. Want order, succession by blood (Adean), the crowning rite. Hate the Infernal Court, Corinite "rebels", later Ravelian pamphleteers [wiki: Regent Court; wiki: Corinite].
- **Corinite state churches** — want evil destroyed now and rulers who earn their mandate. Internal split over magic (Corinsfield bans it; Witch-Kings quote *Corin's Mandate*). Hate Adeanic complacency. Corinite imperial princes made "uneasy alliances" with the Ravelian College of Veridicals against the hardline Adeanic Emperor Galien III síl na Trisfer (he persecuted Corinites from 1666 and Ravelians 1670–1692 — those are persecution dates, not his reign); but once Camir's Corinite empire was in power the Silmuna emperors and Corinite leaders themselves oppressed the Ravelians, one of the causes of the Blackpowder Rebellion [wiki: Corinite; wiki: Ravelian Church].
- **Ravelian Society → Church** — want the Truth, the Fragment, artificery for all, Heaven unlocked. Erudites vs Zealots. Hate "false gods", unlicensed dark magic; by the 1750s run Inquisitions in Sarhal [wiki: Ravelian Church].
- **The Magisterium** — wants a monopoly on lawful magic and a magister in every court. Hates Witch-Kings, shadowmancy and the Shadow Covenant, the Orda Aldresia, artificer upstarts [wiki: Magisterium; wiki: Shadow Covenant].
- **Witch-Kings / Black Demesne adherents** — want rule by power, immortality, the Skull Throne [wiki: Black Demesne].
- **Order of Chroniclers** — neutral recorders who nevertheless print the Mural; pay a "Chroniclers Grant" for relics [eu4: anb_age_of_unraveling; wiki: Ancient Relics].
- **Infernal cults** — Men of the Morningstar etc.; a nation can go Kazraielite at the Castellos reveal [wiki: Kazraiel; eu4: anb_age_of_unraveling].
- **Sun Cult blocs** — Old (hidden temples, Eduz-Nabari guards), New (Jaherian Inquisitors), Jadd (universalist holy war). All can read Castellos' death as vindication [wiki: Old Sun Cult; eu4: anb_religions].
- **Dookanists** — Great Dookan vs Old Dookan vs Bulgu Orazan vs Corinite orcs [eu4: anb_religions; wiki: Corinite].
- **Dragon cults** — Kobold Mallagurtz, Nimrithan, Za'am, Cult of Elkaesal, Korbarid Balakurdinţă; opposed by the Skalds [ck3: anb_religion_dragon_cult].
- **Shadow-Plane powers** — shadow liches, night hags, Red Reapers, fiends [wiki: Shadow Plane].
- **Fey courts** — Seelie, Unseelie, Seasonal; act only through mortal proxies [wiki: Archfey].
- **Order of the Ravenseers** (1022) — wereraven mage-order acting for a "greater good" against the End Times [wiki: Unkind Prophecy].
- **Oni of Azjakuma and the Command** (1444+) — keepers of Korashi; corrupters of High Temples; enslavers of mages [wiki: Damestear; wiki: Rending of Realms].

## 5. Plot Threads with canon basis

1. **"Why did the Silver Dragon not come again?" (1444).** The Greentide destroyed Castellos' own kingdom and he sent nothing. Players can reach the Mural (S1) decades early and choose: publish, bury, sell to the Chroniclers, or hand it to Kazraiel's cult [eu4: anb_age_of_unraveling; eu4: anb_events_religion (corinite.1)].
2. **The empty throne of heaven (1444→).** Adean by blood or Corin by merit; a world-war of temples under blood rain. Twist: Corin's avatarhood rests on a prophecy kept by Agradosian cultists and on her own report of Nerat's words (S18); the Ravelians later say Corin was "but a powerful mage gifted with the ability to naturally wield magic", and make her a saint [wiki: Corinite; eu4: anb_events_religion (ravelian.11.dc)].
3. **The Defiler's twenty years (1022).** Nichmer is dead, but Canrec is alive with a plague that will rise in 1040. The Ravenseers are paranoid seers of an "End Times" (that they foresee *this* plague is an extrapolation [unverified]); Garion, Masilda and Melba will be the ones to cure it, with Ravenseer help, at Varlosen; Alaria's healing is gone. A campaign can end by preventing, containing or weaponising the White Pestilence — and decide whether vampires come to exist [wiki: White Pestilence; wiki: Vampire; wiki: Unkind Prophecy].
4. **The Birthplace of Magic (any era).** In 1022 only a diamond symbol in precursor texts; in 1444 a race across Noruin; in 1836 a vault the Church will not open. Core question: is it God, a prisoner, a predator, or a recording? [eu4: anb_precursor_fragment; wiki: God Fragment; wiki: Ravelian Theurge].
5. **Heaven is locked (1836).** Celestials are orphans; Torrieth walked into the Celestial Planes in 1666; the dead are sorted by Red Reapers in the Crumbling City; belief feeds power. A world-fate plot: find Torrieth, find the gate, decide whether it should open [wiki: Ravelian Church; wiki: Shadow Plane].
6. **The last Tearfall (1836).** The comet is gone; black damestear annihilates magic; artificers need damestear. Whoever controls remaining deposits (Moonisle, Adráil's Celestial Damestear, Aelantir), planar "aerated damestear", or expeditions into the Astral "to extract any damestear that landed on other planets" decides whether magic dies [wiki: Oracle's Eye Comet; wiki: Damestear].
7. **Ducaniel's heirs.** Orcs learning their god was an elven war-mage (S3); the Elfenbride possibly alive; her hidden son Rinetrin, who settled in the forests of Old Rzenta; sealed precursor colonies that may "see fit to return" (S15) [eu4: Flavour_ShatteredCrown_H72; eu4: anb_precursor_fragment].
8. **The sleeping dragons.** Balris wants a benevolent dragon empire; Tayekan may be somebody important in disguise (an in-world theory); the kobold hoard-prophecy; the Lullaby that must never stop; Malazmur hunting towers [wiki: Dragonwake; ck3: anb_religion_dragon_cult].
9. **The war under the world's skin.** Shadow liches versus night hags over whether the Shadow Plane consumes the material one; the Eternal War; Bulwari souls owned by Amussu; the Empire-In-Shadow [wiki: Shadow Plane; wiki: Silent Garden; wiki: Empire-In-Shadow].
10. **Mages' Peace or Skull Throne.** The same idea — mages should stop the wars — produces Anbennar in 1221, the Black Doctrine in the 1500s, Camir's empire in 1700 and the blueblood regime the Blackpowder rebels shoot down [wiki: Mages' Peace; wiki: Black Demesne; wiki: Damestear].
11. **The temples that held the spirits (1444–1836, Haless).** Every relic-hunt in a High Temple pulls a pin; the Oni are draining the hearts; when Tianlou falls in 1751 the Great Spirits come home [wiki: Rending of Realms; wiki: Great Spirits].

## 6. Everyday & sensory detail

- **Calendar (Reformed Castanorian; the wiki has a full Calendar page).** Months: 1 Castanmark, 2 Esmarment, 3 Bloomsdawn (spring; the year began here before the Day of Ashen Skies), 4 Silversight (the Castanites' sighting of the Silver Dragon), 5 Halament, 6 Suren, 7 Teysuren (Bulwari "Sun's Start"/"Second Sun"), 8 Yshdament (Yshtralania and the Dame; harvest), 9 Bloomsend (autumn), 10 Tearfall (damestear meteor season), 11 Nerament (the original last month), 12 Truefrost (added during Gerudia's Era of Frost). So Corin was born 23 Bloomsend (September) 1420 and died 11 Nerament (November) 1444 — the EU4 start date. Weekdays, Monday to Sunday: Newday, Intirsday, Middlesday, Akursday, Faladay, Cedesday, Sorrowday (Cedesday and Sorrowday are rest days; five of the names come from the Kheteratan founding myth of Elikhet, and Newday and Middlesday were added by Castan VII the Preserver). Eras: BA / AA, the "AA" usually omitted. Holy days: Dragonsight (Castellos), Lansday (the Dame, Munas — commemorating the elves' landing on Damesear on the night of 8 Nerament 1000), Corinfall, Nathalfall, Dookansday [wiki: Calendar; wiki: Munas Moonsinger; wiki: Corin (person); deity infoboxes].
- **Funerals by patron:** Balgarites in grand tombs, Munasi to the waves, Damish by moonlight, always with a priest of Nerat present; dwarves light candles at clan shrines on **All Ancestors' Day**; elves keep heirlooms and ash diamonds; Ynnic nobles go downriver on barges; Agradosian cults build great bonfires and hurl their own members' blood on them while a priest recounts the person's valour (a purification rite, not a funeral); some cults cremate their dead; and, as a separate rite, storytellers in god-masks recite the Clarionate and Godswar myths [eu4: anb_age_of_unraveling; wiki: Dwarven Ancestor Worship; wiki: Elven Forebears; wiki: Ynn River Worship; wiki: Cult of Agrados].
- **Agradosian heterodoxy to overhear:** the sun is Draxos' ripped-out heart; the moon is Yshtralania's remains; every god has an infernal "beast side" (Nerat/Canturian, Falah/Mesner, Ryala/Hedine, Balgar/Ildran, Esmaryal/Ibberal, Ara/Forsara) [wiki: Cult of Agrados].
- **Mage life:** warding castles was "steady work for many beginner mages" from the 11th century; "many a Cannorian princess retreats daily to the forested areas… attracting a flock of small woodland creatures" — enchantment practice; Longstrider is cast with a pinch of dirt, a coiled spring, or a shoe sole ("No one likes the shoe-casters"); the first thing a novice conjurer summons "is a friend"; elementals arrive "pre-sentient… and extremely upset"; mage duels under Camiran drill look like the Castonath children's game **throwstone**; graduation once required adding something new to magic, later only fifth-level spells and a dissertation per school [eu4: anb_magic_spells; wiki: Camiran Doctrine; wiki: Cannorian Magical Education].
- **Foci:** staves for broad strokes, wands for wrist-casters, rings and bracers for discretion; bare-handed casting is expert work — "a slight finger in the wrong place may end up with a loss of a hand" [wiki: Spellcasting focus].
- **Damestear in the hand:** a cube or octahedron glowing teal; the mana potion is dull blue and tastes foul; miners cough with Blue Lung; a mage near a rich crystal stares like a drunk at a bottle; to a mage black damestear looks like "a pitch-black nothingness, a void", to anyone else like shiny gunpowder; enchanted armour whose damestear cracks may freeze onto its wearer [wiki: Damestear].
- **Tavern arguments by era.** 1022: was Caylen Longlance truly Adean's avatar; should elves be teaching peasants magic; is the Sorcerer-King's book burned. 1444: is the Escanni adventurers' conclave a real council; is Corin Agrados come again or an infernal trick. 1520s: "what kind of funeral do you give a God?"; the **Lovelost Schism** over whether Corinite couples need Ryala's rite. 1836: is the comet a dead god; do theurges make the Fragment a warlock patron; "Your God is Fake, Good Sir!" [wiki: Avatar; eu4: anb_events_religion; eu4: anb_cannor_religious_flavour; wiki: Oracle's Eye Comet].
- **Deepwoods rules:** stay inside a grove or on the Vyrs; carry a detection charm or prepared detection spell; use the Gladeways with a local guide; expect more fey the closer you get to the Veil [wiki: Verdant Veil]. (The "rabbit that does not belong in these parts" is *not* a Deepwoods detail: it is how the expedition finds the Elfenbride's manse in Old Rzentur, North Aelantir [eu4: anb_precursor_fragment (fragments.306)].)
- **Bulwar:** walled paradise gardens as theology; shades drawn to sunstones; a Kašra's temple is also bank and court [ck3: anb_game_concepts; wiki: Silent Garden].
- **Gerudia:** every small temple is a public library; chiefs recite the fitting Tale before work [wiki: Skaldhyrric Faith].

## 9. Source conflicts (and which was followed)

- **Precursor chronology.** The wiki page carries a newer timeline (War of Broken Chains c. 10,000 BA; orcs 6000 BA) — itself headed "notes and not to be taken as canon" — and an explicitly "deprecated" one (empire founded c. 50,000; Dalaire frozen by Aldan in 1 BA). Followed the newer one; it matches the mod's "seven and a half thousand years ago" and the Dwarven Pantheon page's 6000 BA.
- **When the off-world colonies sealed their portals.** EU4 fragments.606: "many millennia before even the Ruin"; wiki (Precursor Empire, "Empire Amongst the Stars?"): "a century before the Ruin". Followed the mod.
- **Eleventh Pantheonic Council.** Pantheonic Councils, Corinite, Castellos and Timeline pages plus the EU4 event: held 1520, ratified Castellos' death, named Adean successor. Regent Court page: "abandoned due to disagreement on Castellos' death and succession". Followed the majority.
- **Battle of Trialmount.** 1020 on the Timeline and in CK3's game concept; 1021 on the Calindal page; the White Pestilence page says the first case (3 Castanmark 1040) fell "exactly 19 years after the end of the War". Followed 1020.
- **Camir's dates.** Witch-King table "1687 to 1730"; Timeline: unifies Escann 1687–1689, War of Camiran Conquest 1690–1704, Dove Throne 1692, dies 1730; Corinite page says the war for the Empire lasted "only two years". These reconcile if 1692 is the coronation and 1704 the end of mopping-up.
- **Magisterium founding.** Infobox 1284; Mages' Peace page "founded alongside the Empire" (1221); lead text "formed secretly during the Interregnum".
- **Adean's mother.** Second Council: Halanna. Fifth Council, deity pages and EU4: Esmaryal. Use Esmaryal for any date after the Fifth Council.
- **Adelar síl na Whistlehill.** EU4: a floodborn born during the Deluge; wiki: wrote during the Deluge and died in the late 1500s.
- **Cause of the Ruin.** Wiki: Ducaniel's ritual. Mod: the Mural shows only Castellar and "The End"; the Castanor event names "Ducaniel's Wrath"; the Glowing City legend says "the angry gods awoke the earth". Presented Ducaniel as cause and Castellar as failed containment.
- **Corin's birth year.** Infobox 1420 (aged 24); Avatar table "1424–1444". Followed 1420.
- **Seventh Pantheonic Council.** Dated 1015 (Munas) on the Pantheonic Councils and Regent Court pages; but the **Timeline lists "1022 AA — Seventh Pantheonic Council: Munas Moonsinger is deified"**, the Avatar table says Caylen (d. 1020) was confirmed "1022, via the Seventh", and the same page has the Seventh Council weighing Alaria Lifehand (d. Trialmount) and even Aldresia (d. 1053). A 1015 council would have sat in mid-war, only two years after Munas fell, with Caylen and Alaria still alive (the Avatar page speaks of both as dead); 1022 fits a post-war council and coincides with the CK3 start year, but neither date is confirmed over the other. Aldresia's mention must belong to a later council. Flagged; writers of the 1022 campaign should treat the Council as current events.
- **Dookan's nature.** EU4 `dookan_desc` asserts his "true nature is that of the god of mercenaries" (Regent Court view); orcish flavour events assert the Ducaniel origin. Both kept as faction beliefs.
- **Ravelian debate outcomes.** Wiki gives one history; EU4 lets the player decide. Use the wiki's outcome for 1836.
- **Spelling.** The wiki has "Cerisian Sisterhood"; the EU4 event title is "The Ciresian Sisterhood".
- **Chinese terms.** Magisterium is 魔导院 in the user's own game but 魔法师议会 in the EU4 Chinese localisation; Adean 阿德安 vs 阿迪安; Black Doctrine 黑色教义 vs 黑暗教义; Day of Ashen Skies 灰烬之日 vs 灰烬天空之日; the fan bible spells Castellos 卡斯特罗斯. The user's own strings should probably win.
