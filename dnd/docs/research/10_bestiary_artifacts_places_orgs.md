## 1. Overview

Anbennar's monsters are leftovers of named catastrophes. The fey of Escann are scarce, driven into hiding and (some of them) vengeful because Castan II Beastbane burned the World Tree at Oldhaven in 579 BA — the burning *reduced* the fey in Escann, it did not release them [wiki: Castan Beastbane; wiki: Centaur]; vampires exist because crusaders opened one tomb in Corvuria (this is the version revealed in 1900; until the 17th century the public belief was that the god Sur cursed the crusaders for desecrating his temple [wiki: Vampire]); zombies because one acolyte, Canrec the Defiler, engineered a plague; the Serpentspine fauna because a gas called Halanna's Breath seeps up from dead megafauna in the mantle (the prevailing "Megafauna Theory"; old dwarven doctrine says it is the buried earth-goddess Halanna herself [wiki: Halanna's Breath]); the Effelai jungle because, it is speculated, a Precursor flying city crashed. Attach every encounter to its cause.

The mods provide a ready-made encounter framework. EU4's **"Adventurers Wanted"** system infests provinces with one of fifteen threats, each of which grows, migrates, bargains with estates, or is cleared by adventurers, soldiers or "monsters" [AW]. Vic3 keeps the system in 1836 as "Quest" buildings: Brigand Army Base, Lich Lair, Precursor Base, Sea Monster Nest, Den of Beasts, Undead Mob, Planar Breach, Kraken Attack, Fey Incursion, Awakened Treants, Ancient Golems, Thieves Guild, Hag Dens, Eldritch Cult, Vampire Family [V3-AW]. Adventurers are a recognised profession in all three eras; in 1836 they are literally a pop type ("Do-gooders, glory-seekers, sellswords and those struck with wanderlust") [V3-AW].

Relics follow one stated rule: scattered small finds are a romance of skalds and pulp novels; real relics come out of giant deposits (warehouses, buried cities), and even a minor one earns a **Chronicler's Grant** [wiki: Ancient Relics]. By the late EU4 era Precursor relics are a traded commodity that runs dry [eu4: localisation/anb_precursorrelics_l_english.yml].

### 2.2 Bestiary by region

**Western and central Cannor (surface)**

- **Wyverns.** Two-legged dragon-kin with a poisonous barbed tail, usually brown to muddy red; once common across the Khenak Range, near extinct by the Lilac Wars, revived by the Duchy of Verne. Vernmen wyvern riders descend from the first rider, **Armoc Vernid**. Only wyverns raised from hatching and enchanted to see one rider as kin can be ridden; the enchantment is a secret of Verne's royal mages, works for one rider and one beast, and a rider who loses nerve is thrown and eaten. Verne never had many riders; wyverns were "just as valuable for reconnaissance as for battle", and some riders raided deep behind enemy lines. Wyvern venom is the "ultimate solvent of anything organic", hunted by alchemists; Hillthrone gnolls coat weapons with it [wiki: Wyvern]. Trial 7 of Castan is "Befriend a wyvern" [wiki: Trials of Castan].
- **Griffons.** Serpentspine foothills near Marrvale. Prince **Ulric**, son of King Marr, killed the adults and raised the hatchlings; Griffon Knights came to dominate Marrodic society. Breeds: *Swiftclaw* (small, forest scouts of the Marrodic Survey Corps), *Ironclaw* (armoured, screams while diving), *Royal* (largest, weak hearts; King Gryf the Young rode **Seovol**), *Pygmy* (dog-sized pets and hunting hounds of the aristocracy; **bred only in the late 1700s** by Lord Randall Hakebourne, so 1836 only) [wiki: Griffon]. The Marrodic kingdom itself dates from 492, when Ulric tamed a griffon on the trek east [wiki: Dragonwake]. Later mercenary lists add Griffon Sky Lancers, Bombardiers and Grenadiers [MERC]. The **Hendon Terror** is an unexplained cryptid that killed the Marrodic king Edric V 'Ironbeak' [wiki: Hendon Terror].
- **Trolls.** Regenerating giantkin, 2.7–3.3 m, living in packs of 3–12; moral code "My might makes right"; disputes settled by **Zeinbrött** ("bone break": first to rip off the other's arm wins, and the loser is usually handed the arm back). People deal with them by simple contracts: guard this bridge and we feed you. Subspecies: Fjord (Gerudia; mammoth herders awaiting their giant masters), Forest (bridge-dwellers), Cave, Swamp (Yezel Mora; cannot lie outright or break a bargain, and are hurt by cold iron like fey), Hill (Gozengun), and Sea Trolls or "Scrags" (amphibious guardians of the Giant's Grave) [wiki: Troll]. Manticore venom is the one poison that overwhelms troll regeneration [wiki: Manticore].
- **Werewolves.** A fey curse: the archfey *Herald of Change* gave an Alenic warrior the wolf-man form in what was then the Oldwoods. Pre-Castanorian Alenics honoured werewolf lords; Castanor's "Greatwood Cleansings" (449 BA–1 AA) turned them feral and hostile. When the legions left after 1 AA the packs poured out in the **Palemoon Hunt**; the Alenic hunters who drove them back became the **Woodsmen**, who still cull them. Still found in the Greatwoods [wiki: Werewolf]. **Wereravens**: spread by pecking out an eye, only voluntarily; brilliant mages burdened by the *Unkind Prophecy* (foresight that breeds paranoia and hoarding); poisoned by crowberries, hurt by silver; the inner council of Corvuria's Order of the Ravenseers were wereravens. Nichmer the Sorcerer-King was himself a leading Ravenseer who was refused the curse; the order then sided with the League of Free Realms, helped Garion find the Pestilence cure, and was destroyed shortly after the Pestilence by Lucian the Protector (so it exists in 1022 but not in 1444) [wiki: Wereraven; wiki: Order of the Ravenseers].
- **Hags.** Fey witches hated by other fey. *Green hags* (illusionists, self-fulfilling prophecies; the Oldhaven Coven survived Beastbane until the Witch-Kings era; rumoured to live disguised in cities), *Annis hags* (ogre-sized child-eaters who lord over giantkin), *Bheur hags* (solitary winter-bringers of Far Gerudia, kept north by fear of the Skalds), *Night hags* (Shadow Plane, nightmare-senders, century-long schemes, teach necromancy for a price). A coven needs at least three, is stable up to thirteen, and breaks when one member dies [wiki: Hags].
- **Old-Castanor remnants.** Centaurs and satyrs were driven out by Beastbane (centaurs to the Forbidden Plains); minotaurs nearly exterminated; all are "Spawns of Agrados" in Regent Court doctrine [wiki: List of Fey; wiki: Minotaur]. A young **basilisk** is the "worthy beast" of Castan's tenth trial [wiki: Trials of Castan]. The Old Colosseum of Anbenncóst once housed a chimera, the **Dread of Damucen** [wiki: Anbenncóst].
- **Local horrors.** *Balmire marshes*: bodies preserved under the bog knit corpses, plants and mud into a shambling abomination that falls apart again; and "wretched souls contort the landscape to trap unsuspecting victims" (event "Marshes and Will-O'-Wisp") [CM]. *Cursewood* (诅咒森林): shriekers (plain brown mushrooms that split and scream) and older malevolent spirits; tamed only while the enchanted sapling **the Scogtrín** stood, and wild again after the Greentide destroyed it [wiki: Cursewood].

Lesser kin: **drakons** (wingless, red-black, wall-running; from the pit in Gor Vazumbrog, ridden by the Skewered Drake orcs) [wiki: Drake]; **Ninki Nanka** of Fangaula (water-controlling drake with a death gaze that takes up to a week to kill) [wiki: List of Fangaulan Monsters]; **dragonnel** of Nomsyulhan [wiki: Dragonnel]; **dragon turtles** of Insyaa, who are talkative island-sized loremasters (Kealakys the Ever-Hungry eats three feasts a day; Maxele the Iron-Hearted schemes from the Hermit Kingdom of No'hakai) [wiki: Dragon Turtles]. Kobolds believe that when the world's largest gold hoard is gathered at the Dragonheights, **Malliath**, father of dragons, will wake [wiki: Kobold Dragon Cult].

**Giants and giantkin** [wiki: Giants]

Titans (mythical makers), Colossi (about 50 m; drowned in the Giant's Grave Sea when the Last Dragonflight melted Frost King Gerud's ice palaces), Cloud Giants (7–8 m; floating castles still sighted; one city crashed into the Serpentspine during the ogre famine of 1–28 AA, was eaten out by the ogres and became Maghargma City, and another crashed on Insyaa on the Day of Ashen Skies itself, stranding the Skyfall Ogres [wiki: Ogre Valley Timeline; wiki: Skyfall Ogre]), Fire Giants (Forbidden Plains, around volcanic Yarikhoi), Frost, Stone and Hill giants. Their servants survive them: ogres, trolls, ettins (two heads, "monumentally stupid", armoured by Black Orcs), ogrillons (orc-ogre hybrids, mercenary shock troops), cyclops, firbolg [wiki: Serpentspine Fauna; wiki: Giants]. Large humanoid bones really do litter the Giant's Grave Sea floor [wiki: Drowned Giant Isles].

**The Deepwoods (深林) and the fey** [wiki: Fey; wiki: List of Fey; wiki: Archfey; wiki: Ashentree Fey]

- Courts: **Seelie** (the Fey Queen; revel, mischief), **Unseelie** (the King of Darkness; endurance), four **Seasonal Courts** (worshipped in Eordand), and minor courts such as the Dawn Court (the Manic Earl), the Cinderborn Pioneers (the Ashen Hierophant; charred treants) and the Bristlecomb Library (the Steward of Memory: "Death comes for us all, but it cannot claim knowledge").
- Rules of engagement: no fey gives a true name (Nathalyne stole the satyr archfey Dolumir's power with his); archfey never strike each other directly ("the Accord") and always act through pawns.
- The **Ashentree Pact** fey, who "seem to consider" the archfey Blackened Prince their leader (it is unclear whether they are an organisation or only an ideology), want the forest restored across all Escann and a new World Tree planted, and are blamed for "tragic accidents" to explorers and merchants. Legend makes the Blackened Prince the fey warrior who duelled Beastbane for the World Tree and lost [wiki: Ashentree Fey; wiki: Castan Beastbane].
- Creatures: pixies (bandits bait them with wounded travellers), dobies (house-fey; break the bargain and the house burns), boggles (bribe with fruit), **treants** (temperament depends on how the tree was treated), dryads (trespassers "are rarely seen again"), spriggans (discarded servants), changelings (children of firstborns given to the Trader of Masks), kelpies, bonehoarders (induce thoughts of self-harm in the Bone Grove), carapellars (iron-shelled slugs that bisect a man), eyrpiercers (pack hunters whose scream bursts eardrums at sixty paces), giant moths goblins can ride, **cualebris** (winged river-serpents of the Deruwren whose scales turn magic) and **unicorns** (their hearts have "potent magical applications").

**The Serpentspine / Dwarovar (蛇脊山脉)** [wiki: Serpentspine Fauna, quoting Grimmar Gordwar's "Serpentspine Flora and Fauna, 1st Ed, 1732"]

Everything underground lives off **Halanna's Breath** and bioluminescent **Serpentbloom**.
- *Beasts*: bnunin (large aggressive cave rabbits), hold hogs and rothé (dwarven truffle-pigs and cattle gone feral, found near gas vents and ruined farms), **deep crows** (giant crows with scissor mandibles and clustered red eyes; "Ancient" ones command the rest), **hook horrors** (pack hunters; Black Orc clans use them as war-beasts), **purple worms** (their tunnels may be much of the cave system; a fringe theory says Aul-Dwarov used them to dig the railways), grue scavengers, cave crabs "as large as a small cottage" with glowing fungus on their backs, the pink water-running **Logrhundr**.
- *Constructs*: Aul-Dwarov **golems** still guarding vaults, armouries and thrones that may be empty; **living crystals** that pulse like lungs and may "infect" flesh.
- *Oozes*: gelatinous cubes (dwarven folklore says they were made to clean the rails), and the rare **Healing Slime** that mends bones.
- *Others*: flail snails (anti-magic shells; wail when maimed, which draws predators), **Veladklufar** (near-invisible roaches whose jaws cut stone and metal), lumimoth swarms, **Ironweave Skitterers** in the Hul-Jorkad sewers (webs take hours to cut with steel), fungrum, **norkers** (rock-skinned raiders round Gor Bûrad who believe life is a cruel test by their gods), and **Deep Goblins**, pale refugees who will say only that they fear "those below".
- *Undead*: **Echoes** (non-hostile imprints of dwarves who died defending their holds; react to descendants, old songs and the sound of craftwork); **feral vampires** made by the **Bloodscourge**, a hook horror that drank vampiric blood after the fugitive third-generation vampire Erlac of Ioan's Ford fled Corvurian knights into the mountains (1114–1116); it forces lesser beasts to drink its own blood, making Gorebeaks (hook horrors) and Clawthirsters (norkers) [wiki: Bloodscourge]. Orcs call the undead **Orûmshuz** and fear being forced out of the cycle of reincarnation [wiki: Orûmshuz].

**The Primeval Serpentdepths** (beneath the Dwarovar; a late-game or 1836 frontier) [wiki: Primeval Serpentdepths]

Caverns on a sea of magma where *mantle whales* swim, *mantle serpents* hunt them, and *Zirakant* scavengers hibernate in magma and wake in a rage. The **Obsidian Empire** of exiled blood-rune dwarves holds seven holds under the **Runefather** on his obsidian throne at Hargûn Ruzazir ("not even slaves may grace the halls"); it invades the surface through Verkal Skomdihr, and by 1836 an "Obsidian Invasion of the Serpentreach" is a live crisis in which failed generals are "sent to the Runefather to face judgement" [vic3: localization/english/journal_entries/anb_obsidian_invasions_l_english.yml]. Other powers: fungus-folk **Kantharos** who hijack mantle whales as living ships (their serpent-city **Mycos Tharosis** is a moving trade hub); minotaur **maze-cities** (Varian Siram; Nekha beside the Sea of Flowers, whose flower-maze lasts exactly four days, 15 hours and 17 seconds); **Fire-Blooded** pyromancer minotaurs; the devils' **Court of Exiles** under the **Hostess Queen**; the **Mindless Hordes** of broken fiends; the walking volcano **Arzakathn**, whose magma-fleshed children trade under three rules (no ship stays more than three "breaths"; every deal is sealed in blood; no outsider climbs to the upper reaches). The Deepcity **Nixzar** teleported itself into the Middle Dwarovar around 1700.

**Bulwar and the Salahad**

- **Šedukassi** ("Šedu-made" wish-creatures): the *Almiraš* (yellow carnivorous hare with a blue horn that dispels minor magic; a status pet for Surani mages), *Bašmu* (horned winged snakes on the Serpentspine slopes), *Chamruš* (eagle-winged dogs most Bulwari and sun-elf magi keep), *Umu Dabrutu* (bull-bodied, snake-headed fliers, hunted nearly extinct under the Jadd Empire) [wiki: Šedukassi].
- **Lamassu**: bull- or lion-bodied outsiders from the Plane of Air, once the djinn's palace guards, later teachers of the first Bulwari magi; cannot breed here; their elder **Lahmaš the Wise-master** lives on the lapis mountain **Mount Lazzaward**; their script is cuneiform [wiki: Lamassu; GP]. **Manticores** are lamassu twisted by the djinn Al-Kuhun; all male; they reproduce by poisoning and breaking a kidnapped man over months, and the new manticore often kills its old family to dull the memory. One, Ašaharu, ran a gnoll pack on the middle Suran [wiki: Manticore].
- **Bayšigal** ("whisperer demons"): bodiless possessors known only in Bulwar, expelled only by the **Kašipti** exorcists [wiki: Bayšigal]. **Irhungal**, "the Whisperer", is a shapeshifting immortal mage who returns with a new face each time he is killed; the Sun Cult calls him the corrupted magic-god Ešubas [wiki: Irhungal].
- **Genies** (Djinn, Marid, Shaytan, Efreet, Silat, Orisha) cannot be killed, only banished or bound [wiki: Genie]. **Gnoll Xhazobines** are warlocks of the *Xhazob* demons created by mass sacrifice; the First rose from the Burning of Kheterat in 666 BA [wiki: Xhazobkult]. The **Deep Devils** of the Onslaught (c. 1200–1134 BA) are a half-forgotten aquatic confederation that drowned the old coastal empires [wiki: The Onslaught].

**Sarhal**

- **Yezel Mora** (暗影沼泽), the Shadow Swamp: a monochrome wetland ruled through swamp trolls by the **Coven of Yezel Mora** — *Hireet Smoketusk* ("Mama Hireet"; her disguises are always too big), *Ilfanill Gloomweaver* ("Granny Ilfa"; too many or too-long fingers, hidden in gloves), *Komina Riftwhisper* ("Auntie Komina", "the Grief-Eater"; rotten teeth behind a jasmine-scented veil, a closed-lip smile). Their goal: amplify Sarhal's grief to merge the Shadow Plane with the world during the alignment of 1555–1777. They raised **the Vile One** as a child-weapon. Hireet was worn down with Black Damestear artillery and killed by the Mengi hero **Ayana** at Sprigi Twani in 1777; the other two vanished [wiki: Coven of Yezel Mora; eu4: localisation/Flavour_Umbral_Covenant_S90_l_english.yml]. At the swamp's heart stands the strangler-fig world-tree **Shadowroot Matriarch (Yezelne Nisha)**, defended by **shambling mounds** born from its shadow-figs [GP; wiki: Shambling Mound]. A lost **tchoken** (a stupid, vivid, magic-immune farm lizard) is the only colour in the swamp [wiki: Tchoken].
- **Fangaula**: Asanbosam (iron-toothed tree-ambushers made by the Shaytan to guard ironwoods), Eloko (bell-ringing grass-bearded man-eaters), Kadda (half-demon giantkin whose hunger is contagious), Devouring Gourds, hydras of the Dao Nako, Guiamala (seven-horned 20-foot grazers), Aziza fairies served by invisible hands [wiki: List of Fangaulan Monsters]. **Elemental Beasts** (totem animals; Brother Hornbill and Grandmother Water Buffalo are among the lost) can manifest only once a year since the Day of Ashen Skies and die permanently if beaten [wiki: Elemental Beasts].

**Haless and the Forbidden Plains**

- **Jiangshi** ("energy vampires"): scholars and eunuchs who fled Jaher's invasion into the abandoned oni temple **Heiyukcheng** and learned to drain chi; made by the two-part rite *Teimotiji* (Soul-Stripping, Soul-Stitching); once the state of Jinqiu, whereabouts unknown since its collapse and partition; tolerated by neighbours for their service against the invading Sun Elves. Jaher's Eastern Campaigns run 1038–1127, so there are no Jiangshi in the 1022 campaign [wiki: Jiangshi; wiki: Jaherian Eastern Campaigns]. The **Demon Hills** are named for the Oni and are the main source of Black Damestear ("Korashi") [wiki: Demon Hills].
- **Rocs**: third-largest flier after mature dragons and skywhales; nest in the Serpentspine and hunt across the Plains, taking horses and mammoths; centaurs read a roc overhead as an omen of the wind-god Irdaeos and leave chained animals on ground-glyphs as offerings [wiki: Roc].

**Aelantir**

- **The Effelai**: the jungle of northern South Aelantir is a single organism with a nervous system, probably an escaped Precursor experiment from the crashed city of Affelai; it absorbs other plants and, in 1836, people ("Those who burned the jungle shall regrow it" — outsiders become **Seedthralls**) [wiki: Effelai; vic3: localization/english/systems/anb_seedthrall_l_english.yml].
- **Bloodgroves**: trees weep red sap in winter; the Kwineh swear oaths there, and non-sapient degenerated elves, the **Bloodfeeders**, kill to drink [wiki: Bloodgroves].
- **Challúmata**: pale-bronze guardian statues of the Slaver-Nobles cast from soul-infused "Necrochalchum"; found in uncleared ruins of Andeios; a single banishing or exorcism spell often drops a single statue [wiki: Challúmata; wiki: Chénochallúm].
- **Mechanim**: metal Precursor servants with a carved-damestear core (*Kauga*) holding mind and soul; the Insyaan mechanim reawakened in 1366; in 1836 factory automata worldwide begin to "question who they are and why they are here" [wiki: Mechanim; vic3: localization/english/journal_entries/anb_mechanim_awakening_l_english.yml].
- Named terrors: **Malenvijer**, the centuries-old alligator of Mocvare with the spellblade *Escalynnvec* in its skull ("who pries it free rules the Ynn") [wiki: Malenvijer]; the **Kuukuswlaws** ("water-speaker") that calls sleepers into the bay at Ynnsmouth [wiki: The Kuukuswlaws]; the **Demon Bull** (Upeo Uywa) of Amadia, killed by Qilto [wiki: Demon Bull]; **sandworms** of Haraf (1–50 m, "Gift of Ek'elas") [wiki: Sandworm]; **Decuvie** coyote-elves of the Ekyunimoy [wiki: Decuvie]; **Cuāitlak**, the blood-singing elite of Kotli'komhitzi, wrongly called Insyaan vampires [wiki: Cuāitlak]; feral warrens of **kotakompu** sickle-rats in Taychend [wiki: Kotakompu].

**Undeath: the rules** 

- *Vampires*: **there are none in 1022** — the first were made after the White Pestilence (1040–1053). In-world belief until the 17th century ("Sur's Damnation"): envious, mostly Roilsardi crusader-lords desecrated a temple of Sur/Surael in Corvuria and the god cursed them. The rival theory (first recorded in the 1200s, confirmed only by the Errant-Prince Theodore sína Trinás in 1900): the first was **Ettalinde the Wretched**, an acolyte cursed by all seven devils of the Infernal Court for tricking them; the crusaders slaughtered the monks of a Surael temple to reach her tomb, woke her, and she turned them into the **Errant-Princes** (second generation). No reflection; cannot bear children; killed only by direct sunlight or by a stake through the heart *while resting in the coffin* (elsewhere a stake only paralyses). They cannot cross running water except by boat or bridge, nor enter a home uninvited (the water ban binds only the second generation, the threshold ban only the second and third; later generations are weaker but burn more slowly in sunlight). A badly wounded vampire turns to mist and returns to its coffin. The sire-bond holds the child obedient, usually for under a decade; mortals fed vampire blood become thralls. A vampire who does not feed goes feral (and blood from banks or thralls nourishes less than blood from a hunt). Vampire society keeps the **Masquerade** (four Fundamental Laws) and, from **1629**, a governing **Blood Court**, formed in Corvuria against the **Umbral King** during the First Shadow War (1505–1636) [wiki: Vampire].
- *Liches*: soul in a phylactery; go mad without a regular supply of souls; a starved lich collapses into a **demilich**, a gem-eyed skull that follows tomb-robbers. A **Shadow Lich** binds its soul to Yezel Mora itself [wiki: Lich] (巫妖).
- *Zombie plague*: the **White Pestilence** (1040–1053) began at Ravenhill in Corvuria on 3 Castanmark 1040, when a recently dead man was seen "rising from the open grave and devouring the priest of Nerat"; victims turn deathly white before dying and rising, and their souls were bound to the Book of Canrec as "Bellowing souls". It was ended by the Aldresian Crusade, in which Canrec died. Garion the Wise's cure (made with the Ravenseers at Varlosen) still works on a fresh bite, so the real danger is a new strain [wiki: White Pestilence; wiki: Zombie; wiki: Canrec the Defiler].

**The Trials of Castan (卡斯塔诺 / 试炼山)** [wiki: Trials of Castan; eu4: localisation/Flavour_Castanor_B32_l_english.yml]

A claimant climbs Trialmount from the North Citadel. In order: (1) shout a rally cry the garrison answers (Castan XIII the Quiet); (2) trace Castanor's borders "as they are, and as they SHALL BE" on a cold stone desk (Castan V); (3) forge a gift for the dwarven delegation at a magically preserved forge (Castan III Dwarf-friend); (4) swear at the **Oathstone**, where every Castan's oath is carved; (5) add a stone to the gem-cairn called the "crown of mankind" (Castan IV); (6) settle a feud between two forest spirits; (7) befriend a wyvern; (8) "jump around a rock ten times" (Castan XXII the Eccentric); (9) disarm an immortal golem, which then beheads itself and kneels until you die (Castan VI Giantsbane); (10) hunt a beast and craft something from it (Castan II Beastbane). The oath begins "I pledge to defend my land and my people to the end of my days, but will not bring unjust strife unto the world." Trials 6 and 7 were instituted by Castan XIV the Conciliator and Castan XXXVI Dragonfriend; the Oathstone trial has no named author. The Trials were established in 618 BA after the death of Castan the Progenitor, and late in the empire the Gerudian Ebonfrost dynasty (from Castan XLIX Ebonfrost) reserved them for its own heirs. The Trials were wrecked at the Battle of Trialmount (1021 on the wiki; 31 Oct 1020 in CK3 character history), when the Trialmount Company climbed the mountain, "undertaking the forbidden Trials" on the way, to kill the Sorcerer-King; survivors included Clarimonde, Jaher, Garion the Wise (who lost an arm and a leg) and Istralania Warsinger, and the dead included Caylen Longlance and Urion Starsworn [wiki: Trials of Castan; wiki: Battle of Trialmount]. In CK3 "The Wounds of the Trialmount" must be repaired before Castanor can be restored [ck3: localization/english/anb_castanor_l_english.yml]. In EU4 a restored Castanor runs them again; candidates include "the Plucky Adventurer", "the Castonath Patrician" and "the Naive Peasant", and failures are found "in a ditch half covered in mud". Related regalia: Humac's holy mace, which Castan I kept when he buried his father [GP], and an "Ebonsteel Crown of Castanor" that exists in CK3 only as a placeholder [CK3-ART].

Named for Aldresia, the young squire who led the Aldresian Crusade (1040–1053) against the lich Canrec and died killing him, so the order **does not exist in 1022** [wiki: Aldresian Crusade]. When the Greentide struck in 1424 the order was fighting the Lilac Wars and, like the Magisterium, forbade intervention, so knights deserted (see Sword Covenant). The "Decade of Warring Blades": the unpopular Grandmaster Delian síl Arannen found dead (1454); Otto síl Linevar of the radical "Vanguards" elected, tried to rewrite the oath (no chastity, knights may hold land), was defied by the Bladesingers and resigned in spring 1456; Madalena síl Verne (elected Castanmark 1457; executed three dozen knights for drawing steel inside Arca Aldresia) ambushed and killed by a regiment of her own knights at Valiant Keep (1459); the conciliator Sandel síl Orricande (early 1460) was dismissed by imperial decree in 1462 after a knight was killed in a duel in front of Emperor Lothane síl Wex; then **Castana síl Hardwick**, a Wexonard knight forced on the order by Lothane, purged the factions and restored it (1462–1491). Many knights never forgave Lothane síl Wex for the death of Adénn Skylance (the scandal broke in 1468). The order became strongly Corinite; stripped of guard duty in 1572; after the **Incident at Ebrosfeld** (1580) it became a vampire-hunting order and raided Corvuria in 1601 ("the Dance in the Darkness", half the force lost). It later learned that for 46 years it had been the unwitting proxy of the anti-Umbral vampires who became the Blood Court; in 1639 it renamed itself the **Order of St. Aldresia**, martial arm of the new Ravelian Church [wiki: Orda Aldresia].
- **Order of the Sapphire Eye** (created 1021 by the founders gathered at the Tourney of Grand Victory, on the chivalric code of "the first knight" Caylen Longlance, who **died at the Battle of Trialmount** — so in 1022 the order is barely a year old and its de-facto leader was his former squire Clarimonde until she became Queen of the Blademarches; based at Sapphirewatch on Lake Silvermere; arbitrates "Trial by Chivalry"; other founders: the paladin Ser Devan the Righteous and the elf Urion Starsworn (both also dead at Trialmount), Edmund Fairhand (later raised as the death knight Edmund Palehand in the Aldresian Crusade), Prince Lain of Anor, Prince Wynstan of Cast; Greentide-era members are Ser Cecill and Eledas Silurion, who fought beside Corin, and Laurens síl Place, "a last surviving Knight Brother", who co-founded the Sword Covenant) and the **Order of the Blade Stewards** (keepers of Calindal; those blinded by the Test of the Gleaming Blade become "Blind Masters") [wiki: Order of the Sapphire Eye; wiki: Battle of Trialmount; wiki: Sword Covenant; wiki: Order of the Blade Stewards]. Both exist in 1022, as do the **Knights of the Saltmarch** at Saltfort in Verne [ck3: localization/english/anb_holy_orders_l_english.yml; wiki: Knights of the Saltmarch].
- **Order of the Black Thorns** (Corvuria; not before the Aldresian Crusade of 1040–1053): spearhead of the crusade under its leader Lucian síl Vivin, sworn to the síl Vivin kings, who still count "Grand Master of the Black Thorns" among their titles; openly devoted, in an unorthodox way, to Nerat; a "Black Thorn Tribunal" burns a village; motto in practice "Obliterate, with extreme prejudice" [wiki: Order of the Black Thorns]. **Onyx Legion**: Neratic militants founded in the Borders during the White Pestilence, banished in the 16th century by the high priesthood of Nerat at the Necropolis for accusing Corvurian nobles of being undead; sailed to the Reaper's Coast and founded Neratica (奈拉蒂卡) in Aelantir [wiki: Onyx Legion].
- Others: **Order of the Long Lance** (Lancecastle, Cestirande); **Order of Serondor** (Celliande; knights who command galleys against gnoll pirates); **Order of the Roadwardens** (Sugamber, 1578; pistol-and-sword road patrols); **Wardens of the White Wall**; **Knights Magnificent** (Calascandar); the Ynn's **Vitre Kubern**, Knights of the Dome on antler horses [wiki pages; GP].
- Bulwar: **Order of Judges** (wandering lawmen, seat at Azkabar since 557 BA), **Order of Exemplars** (seat on Elizna), **Order of the Zenith**, and under the Jadd the **Lightbringers** (inquisition), **Jasiéne's Daughters** (nurses) and **Militant Jadd** [HO; wiki pages].
- Corinite-era orders (after 1444): **Gallowspeak Graveknights**, **Carmine Crusaders**, **Corinite Firebrands**, **Knights of the Moonfire** (naval), **Cerisian Sisterhood** (secret women's network), **Broken Tusk Brotherhood** (half-orcs), **Dragonforge Balgarites**, **House of Light**. Infernalist counterparts: **Passionates of Agrados**, **Triadic Devotees**, **the Grudgeknights** [HO].

**The Magisterium** (魔法师议会; the user's earlier game uses 魔导院). Regulator of magic in Anbennar; districts are called "towers"; led by a Grand Magister (Crovan of Vinerick in 1444) chosen by the heads of the eight schools. ## 4. Nations & Factions as playable sides

- **Blademarches (刀锋边区) and the Blade Stewards** — want a worthy bearer for Calindal (1022: keep it; 1444: reforge it in secret and retake Bladeskeep). Hate the Bladebreaker orcs and whoever claims the blade unworthily.
- **Orda Aldresia** — 1444: split between duty to the Emperor and the old vow against evil; later, vampire-hunters. Hate the Magisterium, the Wex emperors after the Skylance affair, and Corvuria's undead nobility.
- **Magisterium** — wants monopoly over magic and relics; hates Witch-Kings, the Iron Sceptre, shadowmancers, and rogue schools such as Konwell.
- **Corvuria (科乌里亚) and the Blood Court** — wants the masquerade preserved; hates the Onyx Legion, Aldresian raiders and feral lines that draw attention.
- **Adventurer companies** — land, pay and a name; hate the kings who abandoned them (Cobalt against Gawed) and each other.
- **Dwarven reclaimers and the Asra Bank** — want the Dwarovkron gems and the holds; hate orcs and goblins, and each other's claims (Arg-Ôrdstun's diamond dwarves would rather destroy the Bloody Gem than see Aul-Dwarov restored).
- **Verne (弗恩) and Marrhold (马尔霍德)** — sky-cavalry powers guarding their taming secrets.
- **Deepwoods elves, Ashentree fey, hag covens** — regrow the forest and bind mortals by bargain; hate Castanor's heirs.
- **Umbral Covenant / Yezel Mora** — merge the Shadow Plane; opposed by the Mengi, lizardfolk naga and nearly all Sarhal.
- **Order of Chroniclers / Shadow Covenant / Ravelian Society** — three rival buyers for every relic and secret.
- **1836**: Obsidian Empire (enslave the surface holds), awakened Mechanim (freedom or revolt), the Effelai (reattach its "missing limb"), the Northern League and the great companies (profit).

## 5. Plot Threads with canon basis

1. **The Wounds of the Trialmount (1022).** The Trials are broken, no Castan reigns, Caylen Longlance and eight other heroes of the Trialmount Company are freshly dead, Calindal just recovered (and still unnamed). A party can repair the Trials stage by stage — forge, Oathstone, golem, wyvern — while the Ebonfrost heirs ("Empire of the Black Dragon") and "Silver Dragon" claimants race them [wiki: Trials of Castan; ck3: anb_castanor].
2. **The plague that has not started yet (1022→1040).** Canrec is alive and a lich; the first corpse rises at Ravenhill in 1040; Garion (maimed at Trialmount: he lost an arm and a leg) and the Ravenseers (wereravens who can foresee it and are going mad) are on stage; Aldresia, the "young squire" who will lead the crusade of 1040–1053 and die killing Canrec, belongs to the next generation (her birth date is not in the sources). The crusade's victory creates the vampires: its landless Roilsardi lords wake Ettalinde [wiki: White Pestilence; wiki: Aldresian Crusade; wiki: Battle of Trialmount; wiki: Vampire].
3. **Shards of the Gleaming Blade (1444).** Varok has broken Calindal and killed its king at Bladeskeep; the shards are "eventually" recovered and the Blade Stewards reforge it in secret for the next successor; whoever unsheathes it without being blinded rules the Blademarches. Canon gives a personal edge: Lothane Bluetusk of the Corintar was fathered on a captive Silmuna by a Bladebreaker orc. That the Corintar, other orc clans, the Magisterium and relic thieves all want the blade is a plausible extension, not a sourced fact.
4. **Seven gems for a crown (1444).** One gem lies in a bank vault in Anbenncóst, another is hunted by the Ruby Company, the Amethyst "could be anywhere west of the Jade Mines" and must be traced through tavern rumour; reforging the Dwarovkron remakes or finally ends Aul-Dwarov.
5. **The Masquerade of Arca Corvur (1444–1601).** Never-ending feasts, wine no barrel of which leaves the palace, a knightly order that burns villages, an exiled legion crying "undead", and the first Aldresian patrol lured over the border by a "kidnapped child".
6. **Three aunties (1444–1777).** The Coven's temptations reach courts across Sarhal; the Vile One may rebel; the alignment window closes in 1777 at Sprigi Twani; two sisters survive into the 1836 campaign as rumours.
7. **The Eye under Damenath.** The Plumwall Society (head: Adelar Longstaff) digs the ruins from 1446 for the Magisterium; the Eye of the Dame gives a year and a day of Witch-King power, then a cataclysm. The wiki's own hint is that it is no longer there but was added to a dragon's hoard (Zaamalot's or Nimrith's). Writer's suggestion, not canon: Nimrith's hoard at Khugdihr was carried off by the adventurers who killed him in 1103, and Zaamalot slept under Castonath. Who is allowed to find it?
8. **Tayekan among us.** A blue dragon "possibly leading a powerful organization" is a canon rumour; pair with kobold hoard-cults and, in 1836, the Darkscales' "Red Dragon reborn".
9. **From below (1836).** The Obsidian legions come up through the Serpentreach while automata wake on factory floors and the Effelai makes Seedthralls; the Deep Goblins' "those below" were telling the truth.
10. **The Horn with eight prisoners.** One broken seal on the Horn of Amilak releases a genie that cannot be killed.

## 7. Uncertainties and source conflicts

- **Corin's gear**: no named sword, shield or armour was found in wiki or mods; only iconography and the bombard Malliath's Breath.
- **Castanorian regalia**: no regalia page exists; only the Keys of Nath, Humac's mace (fate unknown), the Oathstone, the "crown of mankind" cairn and a CK3 "Ebonsteel Crown of Castanor" described as "TODO". The wiki Trials page carries editorial question marks; the order is as tabled there.
- **Calindal's breaking and reforging**: broken *before* the 1444 start, when the Greentide took Bladeskeep (EU4 Bladebreaker idea text); the reforging is undated ("eventually… later… in secret"), and EU4's Bladeskeep monument still calls it "destroyed". Treat it as broken in 1444 with the reforging a secret, possibly still to come.
- **Date of the Battle of Trialmount**: 1021 on the wiki (Calindal, Trials of Castan, Sapphire Eye pages), but CK3 character and title history put it on 31 Oct 1020 with Clarimonde holding the Blademarches from 13 Dec 1020, and the wiki's Galéinn page dates the Treaty of Anbenncóst that ended the war to 1020. Either way it is over, and Caylen Longlance is dead, before the 1022 start.
- **Magisterium founding**: infobox says 1284 (end of the Wars of Rule); the Imperial Academy page says officially founded 1221 at the Formation of Anbennar; "formed secretly during the Interregnum". Not resolved. Its capital is given both as Old Damenath and as Adráil.
- **Orda Aldresia dates**: the wiki gives "winter of 1555" for the Bladesingers' defiance of Otto síl Linevar, evidently a typo for 1455. The Shadowmoon Conspiracy is dated 1567 or 1573 by the wiki's own note.
- **Ruby Crown** (resolved): CK3 says it lay "untouched for 178 years until Lorevarn II"; Lorevarn II founded the Kingdom of Lorent and House Lorentis in 900 AA wearing it [wiki: House Lorentis; wiki: High Kingdom of Lencenor], so in 1022 it is on the head of the reigning King of Lorent (name him from dossier 01/02).
- **Lothane Bluetusk** (resolved) is the same man as "Grandmaster Lothane Silmuna of the Corintar" in [eu4: localisation/Flavour_Clouded_Eye_B24_l_english.yml]: his wiki page titles him "the Orc Bastard of Silmuna", son of Eilís the Blue Silmuna. Do not confuse him with Emperor Lothane síl Wex, his contemporary.
- **Vampire origin**: two in-world accounts (Sur's curse; Ettalinde). The Ettalinde account rests on one interview of 1900 and the wiki's "Protector's Crusade", "First Shadow War" and "Blood Court" sections are developer TODO notes, so their details (Lucian turned, perhaps through his daughter; Blood Court formed 1629) are thin.
- **Bal Mire** and **Bal Vroren** are ruins on the wiki but restorable monuments in EU4. The Vertesk "flying eyes" and "Citadel of Conquest" texts are optional EU4 outcomes, not fixed history. The **Imperial Palace** stands "on the site of old castle Dameris" in GP but in a separate Imperial District founded by Jexis on the wiki; I followed the wiki's district layout.
- **Dwarovkron**: "seven gems" [wiki: Gem of Asra] but mod text names six explicitly (Diamond, Citrine, Jade, Amethyst, Agate, Ruby) and says "other gems do appear"; the hold of the Ruby is spelt "Ovdal Vubi" and "Ovdal Vudi" in different lines.
- **Armour of Yodhan**: dated after all — Elrandar Silverspite found it in 1609 and Kamrayakval's War ran 1612–1627 [wiki: Elrandar Silverspite; wiki: Kamrayakval's War]; where it lay in 1022 and 1444 is not stated beyond Taychend. **Golden Throne's fall**, **Horn of Araš's destruction**: only "18th century" (the Surakeši League revolt); 1836 status inferred from "last known location".
